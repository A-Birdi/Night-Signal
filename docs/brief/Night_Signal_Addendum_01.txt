0.1 CONCRETE DEFAULTS CHOSEN TO RESOLVE THE GROUP'S OPEN QUESTIONS

These are explicit design decisions in this drafted addendum, not assertions
that the user previously supplied every number. Adopt them when this addendum
is approved/submitted unless a later user decision changes them:

D01. Six HUMAN entrants per driving activity and six members per convoy;
     twelve TOTAL active race vehicles, including friendly and opposing AI.
     Twelve is a required tested upper bound, not the default size of every race.
D02. Interpret 'six humans for any activity' literally for this revision:
     a meet instance also admits at most six humans. Existing twelve-bay
     scenery may remain, but it is not permission for twelve connected players.
D03. Add three currency-only Freeplay courses FP01–FP03 to the original 26.
     The revised minimum is 29 distinct base courses, not a paywall inserted
     into the required campaign. Thirty stages per campaign mode stay unchanged.
D04. Any active convoy member with permanent access can sponsor a Freeplay
     course for that event. Other convoy members receive temporary guest access.
D05. Offline includes a complete LOCAL progression profile, not merely a demo.
     Local and Online progress remain separate; connecting does not upload
     untrusted local earnings, unlocks, rank, or competitive records.
D06. Provide three repeatable Team Trials under Challenges. They do not add
     mastery RP or replace any of the 75 required challenge/reward pairs.
D07. Freeplay voting lasts 30 seconds by default, one changeable ballot per
     active convoy member; each ballot is one equally weighted draw ticket.
D08. Keep email/password authentication through the chosen provider. A unique
     public @username is for identity and friend lookup; it is not a second
     custom password database or a requirement for username-based login.
D09. Rejoin permission survives a disconnect until the convoy's leadership
     epoch changes, the convoy disbands, or another explicit revocation occurs.
     It reserves no seat and does not restore a disqualified race entry.
D10. No visible or mechanical vehicle damage. Physical contact still affects
     motion and legal scoring. No scratches, dents, detached parts, damage skins,
     persistent repair costs, or deterioration of purchased visual customizations.

1. HUMAN CAPACITY, LIVE AI, AND EVENT ROSTERS

1.1 Separate the limits

Use separately named, centrally validated concepts, not one overloaded
MaxPlayers variable:
- MAX_CONVOY_HUMANS = 6.
- MAX_EVENT_HUMAN_ENTRANTS = 6.
- MAX_RACE_VEHICLES = 12.
- MAX_MEET_HUMANS = 6.
- Offline play = one local human plus the applicable AI, not new split-screen.

For each allocated driving event:
H + A_friendly + A_opposing <= 12, with 1 <= H <= 6.

Spectators and visual replay overlays are not racing entrants. They cannot
place, affect collisions, generate standings, or receive entrant rewards.
Bound spectator connections to the current convoy admission policy; do not
accidentally create an unlimited spectator server. A reconnect never duplicates
an actor or keeps a disconnected human occupying an active member slot.

Store actor kind, team, driver identity, authority, and entrant ID explicitly.
An AI has a profile and server-owned controls, not a fake authenticated account.
Do not represent AI by duplicating a human's account/card/receipt identity.

1.2 Authored opposition rather than filling every slot

Campaign event definitions specify the opposition, role, and formation. Do not
add AI merely to make the vehicle counter read twelve. Initial authoring rules:
- Early regular races: normally one featured rival, sometimes one support rival.
- Later regular races: normally two or three opponents total; use a larger field
  only where the specific course and story support it.
- Lieutenant encounters: a featured lieutenant with zero to two authored supports.
- Normal and Hard finales: the fixed final rival is a LIVE physical competitor
  at every legal human party size. A six-human convoy can race that rival as a
  seven-car event. Do not force six additional rivals into a dramatic duel.
- Freeplay: leader may choose zero through the remaining legal AI capacity.
  Six humans plus six AI, or one human plus eleven AI, must be supported on
  courses/modes authored for the full grid.
- Team Trials: defined separately; their fixed six-versus-six structure uses
  friendly AI when fewer than six humans enter.

Every counted course must support a safe twelve-car start layout for applicable
race modes, even if its campaign event uses fewer. Use staggered grids, longer
start approaches, or safe authored rollout, not overlapping colliders. Preserve
course identity and actual geometry; do not flatten every mountain road into a
wide motorway just to fit a grid.

The UI shows human, friendly-AI, opposing-AI, and total counts. Freeze the full
roster at allocation. A midload/midrace DQ does not cause automatic AI replacement.
Late arrivals spectate or wait. Human team-success requirements cannot shrink
because somebody disconnects.

1.3 Benchmark and live-boss reconciliation

Keep legal, measured benchmark runs as tuning and comparison evidence. Existing
Normal/Hard participation envelopes and human-success thresholds remain unless
explicitly changed below; recalibrate targets under the new contact rules.
A replay is no longer the featured live rival when all six humans join.

For ordinary stages retain the published distinction between on-track placing
and the benchmark/team objective. The panel must state what actually earns a
clear, not imply every stage is 'finish first' when it is not.

For all four lieutenant encounters and both final encounters, add a live-rival
victory condition. A qualifying human must both satisfy the published mastery/
time target and beat the featured live rival. Normal needs at least one such
human; Hard needs ceil(H/2), minimum one. Individual clears also retain the
original active-participation and support-envelope requirements. A precision
tie may qualify as not slower if the declared event rules say so consistently.

A featured rival that legally fails to finish is beaten by a valid finisher,
but the human still needs the mastery target. A rival missing because spawning,
AI initialization, or the server failed is a broken event, not a free boss win.
Do not invent an impossible target or hidden AI grip to compensate for contact.
Keep temporary debug states out of legitimate progression.

2. COLLISION POLICY AND PRISTINE VEHICLES

2.1 Normal races are not ghost races

Campaign, ordinary Sprint/Circuit, contact Drift Attack, and the Team Trials use
bounded Light Contact as their ordinary policy. Live opposing AI are solid,
visible competitors subject to the same road, barrier, grip, and legal progress
rules. Human-human and team contacts follow the same published policy.

Use stable capped impulses and predictable side contact. No deliberate pit-
maneuver AI, prolonged wall pinning, invisible mass advantages, client-authored
knockback, or bypassing road collision. Evaluate contested contacts on the
server. Prediction/correction must account for real contacts; do not silently
turn all AI into ghosts because the first networking implementation is easier.
Test head-on, side-swipe, rear contact, bunching, pileups, wet braking, and
recovery under the documented network impairment matrix.

Contact does not mean demolition derby. Preserve measured wall-hit counts,
speed losses, reset penalties, and clean-sector criteria. Distinguish vehicle
contact from wall contact; light incidental contact must not count as hundreds
of separate impacts. Keep the authoritative incident debounce.

2.2 Explicit non-contact exceptions

Time Attack: all human cars, live session cars, and recorded targets are
non-colliding with one another. Road and barrier collision remain. No live
opposing AI is added to this mode; target recordings are replays.

Meet arrivals, parked-display presentation, tutorial demonstration ghosts,
short validated reset/spawn safety windows, and fading DQ vehicles keep their
necessary safety exceptions. These are not permanent race-opponent ghosting.
A temporary race safety ghost must be visible, bounded, and end only at a safe
non-overlapping position. It cannot be used to drive through barriers or
repeatedly reset for an overtaking advantage.

Remove an ambiguous 'Ghosted standard race' setting from the default flow.
Offer a plainly named Time Attack/non-contact ruleset instead; its records
and rewards must not masquerade as contact-race results.

2.3 No damage at any point

Do not render dents, bent panels, scratches, paint loss, shattered glass,
missing mirrors/bumpers, broken lamps, punctured tyres, damage decals, smoke
from a damaged engine, or progressively damaged material states. No mechanical
wear, post-impact stat degradation, repair charges, or damage persistence.

Tyre smoke, road skidmarks, brake glow where appropriate, brief contact sound,
optional restrained camera feedback, and clean rain/wet-surface effects are
allowed. They are not persistent damage. The car should retain its customized
showroom appearance in the meet and results, including after a rough race.

3. TEAM TRIALS: GROUP CHALLENGES THAT STILL WORK SOLO

Add a clearly separate 'Team Trials' selection under Challenges. Keep the
original 75 mastery challenges and their 75 cosmetic rewards intact. Team Trials
are repeatable event formats, not three extra RP sources or a way to manufacture
78 supposedly unique mastery rewards.

Ship these three authored trials on suitable existing non-exclusive courses:
- TT_MEAN: six-car team versus six opposing AI; lowest team mean race time.
- TT_BEST: six-car team versus six opposing AI; best valid team finishing time.
- TT_DRIFT: six-car team versus six opposing AI; highest combined RAW drift
  score over a defined route and event window.

The player side always has six positions. H humans plus 6-H friendly AI fill
it. The opposing side has six live AI. All H=1..6 configurations are playable
without another human. Use suitable main-roster allies, never R40/R48, and
distinguish teammate colors, names, cars, and score contributions clearly.
Allow a published difficulty choice, not a hidden catch-up multiplier.

Keep roster, team membership, car caps, assist rules, metric, tie policy, and
hard timeout frozen before readiness. These are time/score competitions, not
instructions for AI to ram the other team. Friendly AI follow the same vehicle
controller and traffic constraints as opponents.

Scoring:
- MEAN: each of the six starting positions contributes. Use adjusted legal
  finish time including ordinary penalties. A DNF/DQ contributes the published
  event hard-timeout plus a fixed 30-second failure penalty. Never remove its
  denominator contribution because losing a slow teammate would help the team.
- BEST: compare the fastest legally finished member of each team. At least one
  human must also finish within the published participation envelope for human
  event-completion rewards; an AFK human cannot collect money for an AI win.
- DRIFT: sum six members' banked, validated raw scores, with the original
  anti-loop/anti-stationary scoring constraints. A DQ's contribution is zero.
  No per-client showcase multiplier enters the team sum. Record individual
  contributions, legal progress and finish status. No scoring by parked bots.

For all three, only active eligible human finishers receive ordinary completion
money. AI never receive wallet transactions, RP, individual challenge awards,
course ownership, or soundtrack entitlements. Team victory may use a declared
bounded placement modifier but not multiply each human payout by six or twelve.
Pure-PvP bonuses do not apply to trials containing AI.

Personal challenge predicates still count personal performance only. A player's
'perfect drift' or personal score challenge cannot be completed using the sum
of five AI teammates. Team bests must be labeled separately from personal bests
and keyed to trial/difficulty/roster composition rules. Trial thresholds need
real legal calibration, not fabricated finished-run times.

4. THE CAMPAIGN MAP AND RIGHT-HAND EVENT PANEL

4.1 Make the campaign feel like a growing region

Present the fictional Shiranami region as an authored illustrated/3D route map,
with topography, roads, settlements, coastlines, passes, and landmarks. It is
not a flat list of generic cards. Show thirty stage-preview nodes joined by
legible progression lines. Distinguish regular, lieutenant, penultimate, and
final stages by frame/symbol/text, not only color. The tutorial can be a separate
linked starting point; it does not become a thirty-first competitive stage.

Each stage preview includes a real authored course image or render, stage
number, concise title, event type, completion/lock state, and relevant rival
identity when revealed. A shared course can appear at two stage nodes without
claiming it is two different base courses.

Reveal more terrain and connections as the appropriate campaign progresses.
Initial view emphasizes Act I and hints at the world beyond. Later acts extend
the map's navigable bounds/detail; do not simply shrink every node to fit the
entire region. Use pan/zoom, act navigation, and a Find Next Stage control.
Preserve camera/focus when coming back. Unlock reveals should be short,
skippable, and reduced-motion-compatible, not a long mandatory animation.

Campaign access still uses each player's contiguous frontier, with the convoy
limited by its least-progressed active member. Hard still requires every member
to have the Normal finale cleared. Buying a Freeplay course, borrowing a guest
pass, or looking at another player's record does not unlock campaign stages.

Local campaign-map exploration need not forcibly follow every leader movement.
Distinguish the leader's previewed stage, the formally proposed event, and the
stage this client is inspecting. Show 'Leader inspecting...' and an explicit
Follow Leader / Return to Leader action. Choosing a personal preview never
changes the authoritative event. Clients who browse independently keep their
camera and can return to the shared preview without losing their own progress.

Do not expose unreached plot beats or the Hard finale's identity through
thumbnails, portraits, track titles, record panels, or network metadata when
the corresponding local story discovery has not occurred. A neutral 'Legendary
challenge' treatment can indicate a locked node. Server validation, not hidden
UI alone, still enforces which stage can launch.

4.2 Panel motion and information hierarchy

Selecting a stage opens a contextual panel from the RIGHT with a quick
horizontal slide, a small controlled overshoot, and a stable settled position.
Initial tuning target: 220 ms total, roughly 6–10 UI pixels of overshoot at
1080p reference scale, scaled sensibly with the layout. Text stays sharp and
upright. It must not oscillate repeatedly or delay basic selection input.
Reduced Motion removes spatial overshoot and uses a brief crossfade/immediate
change. Rapid selection replaces/cancels the old transition rather than
queueing panels. Close and Back remain reachable during animation.

On ordinary desktop layouts the panel occupies approximately the right third,
leaving a useful map view and the persistent convoy/status header. At small
supported window sizes or large text settings, use a readable larger panel or
sheet instead of clipping data or scaling text to illegibility. Do not claim
phone support merely because the UI scales.

Order of content:
1. Stage title, mode/difficulty, actual course/variant, rival/team and conditions.
2. Published objective and what grants a clear, including team requirements.
3. THIS viewer's personal bests and local/online verification label.
4. Compact convoy comparison, normally the top three with expand-to-all-six.
5. Eligible car/cap, lineup/contact summary, access/block reason, and actions.

Leader actions: Propose/Select Event and later Request Ready/Start. Clients see
leader actions as state, not disabled lookalike primary buttons everywhere.
Their actions include Inspect, Car Setup, Ready/Unready when applicable, and
Return to Leader. Neither opening the panel nor selecting a preview starts a
race or spends money.

4.3 Record semantics

Use typed objective/metric definitions, not one unconditional 'Time/Score' pair.
Examples: elapsed time, best lap, raw drift score, clean sectors, perfect-drift
count, target delta, team mean time, team best time, or combined team score.
Show only metrics meaningful to that event, with clear units and targets.
A new 'perfect drift' predicate must be a defined server-tested metric with
one award per eligible judged corner, not an attractive label lacking logic.

Use a consistent race-time format such as mm:ss.mmm, e.g. 03:42.815.
No valid time: --:--.---. No eligible score: 'Score: N/A'. No valid count:
'Best: — / Target: 5', not zero pretending an attempt occurred.
Separate 'No result', 'Not attempted', 'Did not finish', 'Pending verification',
and 'Legacy/incompatible result'. Stage not cleared does NOT mean no record:
a legal losing run can set a personal best. Keep bests visible after clearing.

Compare only compatible records. Store event/stage/trial ID, mode and difficulty,
course/physics/scoring/ruleset versions, direction, conditions, car class/cap,
assist category, contact policy, and team-size/composition policy where relevant.
Store the exact car/build and result provenance with each record. Allow optional
same-car filtering; do not silently compare a ghosted time with a contact time,
Normal with Hard, local unverified data with online verified records, or a team
sum with one driver's personal score.

The convoy summary is scoped to current members and the selected compatible
rules. Do not expose full wallets, email addresses, passwords, or private
inventories. Show rank, handle, best value, car/class, and verification as needed.
Ties remain ties at authoritative measurement precision. Highlight the local
player when present; do not manufacture empty leaderboard entries for others.

Fetch/cache records without blocking map animation. Handle stale, loading,
permission, offline, and error states. Online personal/convoy records are
server-owned; clients cannot publish a claimed PB as verified. Keep old valid
records archived if new contact/physics rules make them incomparable rather
than deleting progress or pretending they were measured on this version.

5. COURSE ACCESS, PURCHASES, AND CONVOY GUEST PASSES

5.1 Permanent ownership rules

Each local/online progression domain has its own course-access ledger. All
content can be installed locally while launch permission remains data-driven.
Do not add DRM, an external asset purchase, or per-race entry fees.

Initial course-access table:
- T00 and C01–C04: available from the beginning in the applicable profile.
- C05–C24: either buy Freeplay access for 45,000 Credits each, OR earn permanent
  access for free by clearing that course's REGULAR Normal campaign stage.
  Derive and store the exact stage mapping from the original campaign catalogue;
  do not mistakenly map a reused lieutenant stage or rely on course-number math.
- C25, the finale route: permanently granted by the first Normal S30 clear;
  not separately sold at launch. Its Freeplay version has neutral staging and
  no campaign-final opponent or finale-exclusive score/music presentation.
- FP01: 45,000 Credits; FP02: 54,000 Credits; FP03: 63,000 Credits. These three
  are permanent Freeplay-only purchases, not campaign unlocks.

The FP prices use an INITIAL reference of about 9,000 Credits for an ordinary
completed race, corresponding to roughly five/six/seven typical payouts.
This is a balancing assumption, not measured economy evidence. Simulate and
playtest using ordinary mixed placement, not first-clear jackpots or repeated
Hard wins. Tune these fixed published prices if necessary and record the
resulting races-to-unlock. Do not personalize prices by a player's wealth or
recalculate them secretly after every race.

Course cards state the alternative clearly: 'Buy Freeplay access — 45,000' and
'Unlock free: clear Normal Sxx — [stage title]'. Currency-only cards say that
explicitly. Owned/starting/rewarded/purchasable/guest-access states are distinct.
No purchase grants a campaign clear, Hard access, RP, a rival identity, or an OST.
A player who bought a route then clears its stage keeps ownership; there is no
second purchase, invented duplicate reward, or automatic refund absent an
explicit future policy. Explain that purchasing is an early-access alternative.

Transactions are idempotent and server-validated Online, atomic locally Offline.
Confirm before spending and show the resulting balance. A simultaneous purchase
and campaign unlock cannot double-charge: if the free entitlement commits first,
the purchase returns Already Owned with no debit. If the purchase genuinely
commits first, the later stage clear just retains that entitlement.

5.2 Do not split friends over a track purchase

A Freeplay event is convoy-accessible when at least one CURRENT active member
has permanent access and the course supports the selected mode. Any such member
may sponsor the event, including a non-leader. Every participant receives a
server-issued, event-scoped guest pass. UI example: 'Available with Robin —
guest access for this event'. No guest receives permanent ownership merely by
participating or downloading the course.

The leader can choose from that accessible union. A non-leader may inspect and
purchase for themselves, but cannot spend another member's money. A successful
purchase updates the convoy-accessible pool and eligibility without changing
anybody's chosen car or force-selecting the new course.

Before allocation, loss of the last sponsor invalidates that course proposal
and any active ballot relying on it; explain the cause and return to a valid
state. Once tickets and entitlement are frozen at allocation, the event-scoped
passes last through that event even if the sponsor disconnects. The sponsor's
DQ does not eject valid guests. New events need a current eligible sponsor.

For an allocated Custom Cup, validate and freeze access for every scheduled leg
up front; already granted cup-leg passes survive the sponsor's departure for
remaining allocated members. Late joiners do not inherit unauthorized driving
entry. Guest participation cannot clear campaign stages or acquire their OST.
Normal event money and legitimate personal mastery challenges remain available
where their specific rules permit the guest's event.

The original 75 challenges must not newly REQUIRE the three extra purchases.
Avoid a challenge saying 'all courses' silently expanding into paid-only extras;
preserve the original explicit base-course set for that predicate.

6. FREEPLAY VOTING AND SHARED TIME ATTACK

6.1 Non-leaders can browse meaningfully

Freeplay is viewable by any convoy member while the convoy is idle/in menus.
A non-leader may inspect unlocked/locked routes, compare cars/records, buy
access, and prepare a nomination. Browsing never grants leader privileges,
forces others into the screen, or changes an allocated event. Active racers
cannot browse away from driving without the existing pause/quit rules.

A nomination before a voting window is merely a local bookmark. An accepted
server ballot exists only during the active voting window.

6.2 Mode agreement, voting, then event agreement

Preserve two explicit consent levels:
- Mode Ready: agreement to enter a particular mode/submode/difficulty selection
  flow with the current convoy roster. For Freeplay, identify Sprint, Circuit,
  Drift Attack, Time Attack, or Cup before opening its course vote.
- Event Ready: agreement to the finalized course, rules, lineup, car cap,
  loadout, conditions, and access after selection/voting resolves.

The Voting toggle is always visible to members in the Freeplay flow, labeled
ON/OFF and leader-controlled. It is disabled for activation until every current
member, including the leader, is Mode Ready. A leader may prepare the toggle
and options but cannot start the ballot while another member is unready.
Mode consent does not pre-authorize a race with unknown parameters.

When voting is off, the leader selects a compatible accessible course directly.
When voting is on, opening a ballot starts a SERVER deadline, default 30 s.
Allow 15/30/45/60 s choices before the ballot begins. Display the countdown and
current mode in the persistent top strip for every member, even while inspecting
a car or walking at the meet. The timer is time to vote, not an involuntary
race-start countdown. Abstaining has no penalty.

One ballot per active human account, leader included. Members may change their
course until the deadline. Show eligible candidates and vote totals. Unsupported
mode/course combinations, inaccessible courses, and stale ballot revisions
must be rejected by the server, not merely grayed out locally. A client with
no personally owned route can vote on a convoy-sponsored eligible route.

At expiry freeze the ballots. The leader can choose a course directly or press
'Draw from Votes'. Clearly distinguish leader selection from a random draw.
No early draw by default; any supported 'everyone submitted' shortcut must be
explicit before consent, not silently inferred from six clicks.

The server chooses one accepted ballot uniformly. Thus three votes for one
course among six ballots give it three of six chances. Display that weighting;
do not promise a uniform draw among unique names while using vote weighting.
Use authoritative random selection once per ballot. No wager, entry cost, or
currency stake is involved. Persist the selected result and ballot revision so
reconnecting or retransmitting cannot reroll it.

Presentation: cycle through voted course previews rapidly, then slow and stop
on the server-selected winner, normally within 3–4 seconds. The animation is
presentation, not the RNG or final authority. Late viewers show the known result
or remaining animation segment. Reduced Motion shows a brief sweep/fade and the
same winner. UI state/timing must not determine which course wins.

No votes -> say 'No votes received' and allow direct selection; do not quietly
draw from locked routes or randomize a purchase. A single voted course resolves
to it. Do not repeat the roulette endlessly to fish for a preferred outcome.
A leader who overrides a draw must explicitly cancel/select a new event; that
change is visible and all Event Ready consents are invalidated.

Roster change, Mode Unready, mode/difficulty change, leadership change, or loss
of all sponsors for a candidate cancels an active ballot with one clear notice.
Preserve useful local nomination choices, not stale server votes. Require a new
mode/roster agreement before reopening. Cosmetic/audio settings do not cancel
ballots. After resolution, freeze the event proposal and obtain Event Ready
from everyone before the normal load barrier/countdown.

6.3 Multiplayer Time Attack

Allow one through six humans to drive simultaneously in Time Attack. Every
human is non-contact with every other human and every target replay. Ghosting
is a server/gameplay collision policy, not just transparent material. They still
collide with road surfaces and barriers; normal checkpoints and resets apply.

No live AI racers are added. Allow personal, friend-shared, or ordinary authored
(non-final-boss) target recordings, with a bounded visible-replay count and
compatibility labels. Players have independent legal times and local replay
visibility preferences; one driver's invalid lap cannot invalidate another's.
Use a shared synchronized start for the convoy session. Multi-lap practice and
reset timing require explicit recorded rules, never an ambiguous global timer.

Show convoy time comparisons and sector deltas. Finish/timeout/spectate rules
remain clear; one slow or disconnected car does not hold everyone forever.
Do not award the pure-contact-PvP winner bonus in Time Attack simply because
multiple authenticated humans are present. Use the declared Time Attack payout
bands, no RP farming, and no ghost-imposed collision.

7. CONVOY INTENT IS NOT CURRENT ACTIVITY

The leader has a persistent, replicated Intent: Campaign, Freeplay, or
Challenges, with Normal/Hard or submode details when known. This is separate
from what the leader is actually doing now. Example:
'Leader: Robin — Garage | Intent: Hard Campaign | Mode ready: 3/6'.

Every member retains coarse actual presence and local screen information.
Do not label a leader standing in the meet as 'In Campaign' merely because the
intent is Campaign. Do not broadcast password/reset screens, typed messages,
private account details, or unsaved fine-grained customization changes.

Changing Intent creates a new mode-proposal revision. Old Mode Ready and Event
Ready states cannot authorize it. All members explicitly agree; the leader's
Enter Mode action commits the transition once they are all ready. Solo uses the
same intent/confirmation semantics without artificial waiting for absent peers.
Entering a mode never starts its event automatically.

Mode-specific leader controls and client views:
- Campaign: leader preview/proposal, shared frontier, right-side record panel;
  clients inspect, follow, compare their own records, and ready.
- Freeplay: voting policy, ballot/draw or direct selection, course access,
  mode options and AI controls; clients vote/inspect and ready.
- Challenges: leader picks a compatible trial/challenge event; clients see the
  metric, eligibility, loaner/team composition and their own relevant progress.

Ready requests reach people in Garage, Meet, Card, or other allowed menus through
the SIGNAL ribbon and a persistent actionable indicator. Do not spam repeat
modals. Keep explicit Unready and preserve local drafts. Shared mode entry must
not discard a paint edit: save a draft or resolve Apply/Discard before consent.
Everyone sees what the leader is proposing, not the leader's full private UI.

8. FRONT MENU, AUTHENTICATION CHOICE, AND REAL OFFLINE PLAY

8.1 Startup identity

Opening the game presents an authored Main Menu front and center, with its own
exclusive title theme, distinct from post-login convoy/menu music. Required
actions: Online Login (or Continue Online for a valid saved session), Offline
Play, Settings, and Credits/Quit as appropriate. Remembering credentials is not
permission to hide the choice or show a fake online status before connection.

An offline/unreachable server must not trap the title in a retry loop. Show
connection state, Retry, and Offline Play. Loading the title and entering an
existing local profile do not require internet, a Supabase account, Docker,
Unity Editor, a separately installed server, or an external runtime service.

8.2 Local progression domain

Offline Play is a genuine single-human campaign/freeplay/challenge/garage mode
with local saves, AI opponents/teammates, the same authored courses/story, local
currency and unlocks, local personal bests, and an offline meet/photo/boombox
space. It is not an online-looking page full of fake friends or connected cars.
Reuse the shared rules/content/vehicle simulation with an isolated local
persistence adapter; do not build a second incompatible racing engine.

Clearly mark the profile 'Local / Offline' throughout relevant menus, wallet,
records and rank. The local card needs a display name and appearance but no
password or globally reserved handle. No online Friends, convoy invitations,
public meet membership, verified online leaderboard submissions, or server RP
writes are available. An optional offline photo space has authored ambience,
not bots falsely labeled as real online visitors.

Local progress and the authenticated Online profile are separate. Offline can
have its own 15,000-RP mastery track but cannot upload that rank, currency,
course rights, records, achievements, OST unlocks or owned parts as verified
online state. Do not silently merge ledgers or accept a local JSON save as a
signed online race receipt. Explain this before starting the first local game
and again when switching domains. This separation is a deliberate launch
tradeoff, not an error or erased save.

A future trusted offline-to-online progression scheme requires a separate design
and explicit approval. Do not invent client signatures with a secret embedded
in the game and describe them as proof of honest offline play.

Save local profiles atomically with schema versioning, bounded recovery copies,
and clear disk-error reporting. Preserve existing online and local data through
updates. Gameplay preference settings may be shared by user choice. A cosmetic
layout blueprint may be offered across domains only as an optional draft; the
Online server must revalidate ownership/compatibility of every referenced item.
No silent stripping of an offline livery while overwriting the online one.

8.3 Go Online and connection loss

Provide Go Online in ordinary offline menus. Pause or finish a local event and
preserve its result before switching; an in-progress local race cannot become
an online reward-eligible race halfway through. Authenticate, establish the
actual control connection, load the server-owned profile, then enable Friends,
convoys, online meet access, and verified record views. Failed connection keeps
the offline profile intact. Never show online success from cached credentials
alone. On return Offline, retain that separate local profile unchanged.

A midrace online connection loss does not silently convert the current online
race into a reward-eligible offline copy. Resolve that entry under DQ/Aborted/
durable-finish rules, then offer Reconnect or Offline Play. Once offline, the
Friends panel is unavailable; cached names must not present false live statuses.

9. FRIENDS, USERNAMES, AND SAFE SIMPLE ACCOUNTS

9.1 A useful, toggleable friends panel

Provide a Friends button with request/invite count where useful. The panel is
closed by default and can be opened/closed on demand from appropriate online
menus and the meet. Do not keep a permanent friends sidebar consuming the car
view. Active driving must not be obscured by unsolicited friend popups; defer
requests to a safe overlay/results view. Offline hides/disables this feature
with a clear Go Online explanation, not a fake populated list.

Add friends by exact @username lookup, showing their public Player Card before
Send Request. Requests require acceptance. Support cancel pending request,
accept, decline, remove friend, and block. These operations are idempotent;
retries cannot create duplicated relationships. Rate-limit abusive searches,
requests and invitations even in a small friend group. Profanity filtering is
not a launch requirement, but length limits, safe rendering and input validation
are still required.

Rows include the public card/avatar, display name and unique handle, connection
status, and permitted coarse activity: Available, Garage, At Meet, Preparing,
Loading, Racing, Spectating, Away, or Offline. Stale/unknown status is not a
false confirmed Offline/Online claim. Friends can inspect cards, invite to
convoy, request/join where permission allows, and Rejoin only when the server
supplies that eligible action. Do not reveal email, IP, access tokens or wallet.

Friend relationships belong to stable account IDs, not mutable display names.
Invitation permissions/capacity, room privacy, block lists, build compatibility,
and current activity are checked on the server at use, not trusted from an old
button state. Race joining remains spectate/next-event only after allocation.

9.2 Names and credentials

Use a distinct public username at online registration, alongside the existing
email/password provider flow. Initial handle rule:
- 3–20 ASCII characters; first character is a letter; remaining characters are
  letters, digits or underscore. Example: Robin_Birdi.
- Case-insensitive uniqueness using a canonical lowercase value and a database
  uniqueness constraint. Keep a display casing separately if desired.
- Strip an optional leading @ for lookup, not as part of the stored handle.
  No spaces, control characters, markup, ambiguous Unicode confusables, or
  system-reserved handles such as admin/system/support.
- Display name is separate and can use supported Unicode, within a 24-grapheme
  budget. Render as literal text, including in Unity rich-text-capable widgets.
- Existing accounts need a safe handle-claim step, not new accounts or a wallet
  reset. Use stable IDs for all friendships/progress after a handle change.

Keep Email + Password for sign-in and provider-supported email recovery at
launch. Do not add a custom username/password credential store or expose an
unauthenticated username-to-email mapping just to make friend lookup work.
Username login is not required by this revision. No passwords for local profiles.

Password policy for new/changed passwords: minimum 15 characters, maximum at
least 64 where the actual provider safely supports it; allow spaces and
password-manager paste. Do not require an uppercase/lowercase/digit/symbol mix,
periodic forced changes, or security questions. Check common/compromised choices
using supported mechanisms without buying a new plan or leaking passwords.
Existing users must retain a recovery path; do not silently lock out all prior
accounts when strengthening registration validation.

Verify the exact deployed provider's character and encoded-byte limits and
apply them consistently in registration, reset, change and login. Supabase's
documented bcrypt storage makes this a real compatibility check: do not assume
64 arbitrary Unicode characters always fit a provider byte limit. Reject an
unsupported value with a clear length explanation; never trim, case-fold,
silently truncate, or invent client-side prehashing to work around it. Do not
claim full NIST compliance merely from these few policy choices.

Provider owns hashing, token issuance and recovery. Keep passwords out of game
logs, crash dumps, analytics, repository files, RPC payload dumps and PlayerPrefs.
Use protected transport, protected refresh-credential storage, generic invalid-
credential messages, endpoint rate limiting, and non-enumerating password-reset
responses. Email verification/reset delivery is a real operational dependency;
development mail capture is not proof that a friend's real email was sent.

10. DISCONNECTION REMOVES MEMBERSHIP, NOT THE OPTION TO RETURN

10.1 Confirmed disconnection versus ordinary latency

Use the master's bounded heartbeat/transport-disconnect handling; a delayed
packet or brief UI hiccup is not immediate DQ. On confirmed loss of the required
server/control connection, remove the player from ACTIVE convoy membership,
clear their readiness and accepted vote, release ordinary seat occupancy, and
publish one roster change. Do not leave an offline member permanently blocking
'all ready' or silently replace them with an AI in the running race.

A dropped gameplay connection during an event also triggers the race DQ rules
and the required membership update, even if another channel remains connected.
Keep actual account connectivity and race participation distinct when showing
'Reconnected to service / Disqualified from this race'.

10.2 Server-owned rejoin eligibility

Before removing membership, record a server-owned rejoin grant containing the
account ID, convoy ID, leadership epoch, reason, and relevant permissions.
This is not a client-invented last-convoy string and not a permanently active
seat reservation. Keep at most the relevant current grant per account.

The grant remains meaningful while that same convoy exists with the same
leadership epoch. Invalidate it when:
- Leadership changes, even if it later returns to the same person.
- The convoy disbands.
- The member is kicked/blocked, its permission is revoked, or account access
  is disabled.
- The user explicitly chooses Leave/Forget Convoy, or joins a different convoy.

Do not add a hidden 60-second eligibility expiry that contradicts this rule.
Short-lived transport/ticket tokens may expire; a valid account can request a
fresh ticket from the still-valid server grant. Capacity can temporarily prevent
rejoining without claiming the underlying prior-membership fact never existed.

After account connectivity returns, the server supplies canRejoin plus a reason
when unavailable. Offer one 'Rejoin [convoy]?' prompt with Rejoin / Not Now.
Not Now dismisses the popup without erasing eligibility. The Friends list can
later display Rejoin for a friend in that same eligible convoy. Suppress
repeated prompts during reconnect retries. Never show an enabled Rejoin action
solely because local storage remembers a convoy name.

Revalidate atomically when clicked. If full, show Full; if leader changed, say
so and permit a normal invitation/request path where allowed. Do not eject a
new member or violate the six-human cap to reclaim a former slot.

10.3 Leader loss and event integrity

If the leader disconnects while others remain, show the existing short leader-
unavailable grace, default 15 seconds after confirmed loss. Disable leader-only
commits during it. If they reconnect and explicitly rejoin before transfer,
leadership may remain unchanged. Otherwise elect the longest-connected eligible
member with stable tie breaking and increment leadership epoch. All old rejoin
grants then fail the leadership condition; the former leader does not seize
control on return. A normal new invite remains possible.

If no active members remain, disband that convoy; do not preserve an empty room
indefinitely just to retain grants. The client explains that there is no active
convoy to rejoin. A system-wide server recovery must use durable validated room
state or mark it unavailable, not fabricate a recovered membership list.

Rejoining restores convoy membership only. During loading/racing, a previously
DQ entrant stays DQ and can Spectate or return to menus. The next event needs
new readiness. The server continues other drivers' race after leader loss.
Pre-DQ durable finishes and reward receipts still settle exactly once; invalid
client claims cannot be converted into compensation or a second entrant.

11. HIGH-ENERGY AUDIO AND AN UNLOCKABLE OST

11.1 Audio identity and routing

Give the opening Main Menu its own unmistakable theme. The signed-in menu,
Campaign map, Freeplay and challenge browsing should be notably energetic:
original eurobeat-inspired hooks, EDM rhythm, and selected rock arrangements.
The Garage/parts/livery editor is less dense and more relaxed without becoming
unrelated sleepy background music. The meet has an inviting default cue and the
interactive boombox described below.

Energy means groove, arrangement, rhythmic articulation and memorable melody,
not simply increased volume or faster random notes. Preserve headroom and
consistent perceived levels between tracks. Menus should remain tolerable for
long tuning sessions. Engine, tyres, impacts, UI, ambience and voice/subtitles
retain independent appropriate mix controls. Muting music cannot mute start
lights, important alerts or driving cues. Avoid piling a menu loop under a meet
track or restarting a song after every small submenu selection.

Recommended minimum cue inventory: 24 genuinely authored arrangements:
- 1 exclusive opening/title theme.
- 2 connected-menu/map/freeplay themes or substantial contrasting arrangements.
- 1 Garage/customization theme.
- 1 default meet theme.
- 1 tutorial bed.
- 2 results treatments: success and loss, not punitive/error-system noises.
- 6 regional ordinary-race themes spanning eurobeat/EDM/rock.
- 4 lieutenant/key-rival themes, one for each encounter identity.
- 1 penultimate mastery theme.
- 1 Normal final-boss theme.
- 1 Hard legendary-final-boss theme.
- 3 Team Trial themes, one per trial.

This is a minimum production target with a scope cost, not permission to count
the same 15-second loop under 24 names. Main loop material should develop for
roughly 90 seconds or more before an obvious full repeat, with musically clean
loop points and intros/outros appropriate to event transitions. Small results
stingers may be shorter. Reuse motifs deliberately, not all instruments and
melodies unchanged. No franchise tracks, recognizable copied melodies, ripped
engine samples, or unauthorized performer imitation.

11.2 Special themes remain special

Lieutenant, penultimate, Normal-final and Hard-final themes play only in their
assigned encounter context and, once legitimately unlocked, the boombox.
Exclude them from ordinary menu shuffle, unrelated races, generic Freeplay,
random boss playlists, and ordinary track previews. Short arrangement stems
used within their own intro/race/outro are allowed, but their identity must not
leak into every event merely because the same course is reused.

R40 and R48 each get a different full theme; do not just speed up or transpose
the Normal finale for Hard. Ordinary course reuse must not trigger a final
boss theme through a course-ID-only audio lookup. Event context controls music.

11.3 Unlocks

Every collectible cue has a stable ID, audio asset/provenance, allowed contexts,
and one declared acquisition rule. No mystery event references or missing
unlock paths. Baseline title/menu/garage/meet/tutorial/results cues are available
in the player's local/online collection from the appropriate initial/tutorial
state. They do not need an impossible 'defeat a menu' condition.

Regional race cues unlock on the first eligible clear of the declared first
Normal campaign stage featuring that cue. Each lieutenant cue unlocks on that
lieutenant's first designated campaign defeat. Penultimate unlocks from its
stage; final cues from Normal S30 and Hard S30 respectively. Trial cues unlock
from the first eligible victory in their named Team Trial. Record exact IDs in
a music-unlock manifest before implementing them. Use the first canonical
source event, not whichever event happens to load earliest on a player's PC.

Merely hearing a cue, viewing a cutscene, losing, listening at somebody else's
boombox, spectating, or buying a course does not unlock it. The Online server
awards the collection entitlement with the relevant eligible result transaction,
exactly once. Offline does the same only in its local domain. OST collection
adds no mastery RP and does not replace any of the 75 cosmetic rewards.

11.4 Meet boombox

Place an original, clearly interactable boombox at an appropriate safe social
spot, such as the tea kiosk/radio bench. Within interaction range, a player can
browse their unlocked cues, see titles and source-event notes, and cycle or
queue a track. Locked entries are silhouettes or spoiler-safe labels with an
unlock hint. Final themes must not be previewed through a supposedly locked
menu. Basic skip/queue controls work with controller and mouse.

The meet has ONE shared playback state owned by the server: room ID, track ID,
queue revision, playback start timestamp, submitting player, and short control
lease. Replicate metadata and play the bundled audio locally; do not stream
someone's computer audio or send PCM over vehicle networking. Late visitors
seek to the current position where supported. Synchronization is social, not
sample-accurate DJ networking; correct drift without rapid audible restarting.

Anyone present may queue music they personally own, even if other listeners
have not unlocked it. Hearing grants no ownership. For public/private friend
comfort, default 'Protect unreached boss music' ON: a listener who has not
unlocked that boss cue may hear the neutral meet bed locally instead. They can
opt in explicitly without gaining the cue. Do not display an unreached final
rival's name in a track title when protecting spoilers.

Serialize controls: short 15-second renewable interaction lease, one queued
selection per person, a bounded queue (maximum six requests), and a minimum
10-second room-wide interval between user-triggered track changes. Provide a
clear queue/Now Playing view, not race-condition 'last packet wins'. Closed UI,
out-of-range, disconnect, or changing activity releases control. A track already
playing may finish after its submitter leaves; remove that person's unplayed
requests and choose from remaining valid requests afterward. If none remain,
return to the default meet cue.

Local mute/volume, leaving the audible area, spoiler protection, or entering a
race cannot stop music for everyone. Fade the boombox appropriately with distance
and mix the room's fallback bed without two full-volume tracks competing.
Offline meet uses the same interaction locally without pretending other people
are connected. Audio unlocks, timing, and controls must survive reconnect without
repeated notices or abandoned AudioSources.

The requested 'parody' tone can support original affectionate jokes about

  deadline, empty pool, single candidate, weighted votes, reconnect, leader
  override, membership change, and stale packets all produce coherent outcomes.
- The same frozen random selection reaches all clients regardless of animation
  speed, dropped frames or reduced motion. Reconnect cannot reroll.
- Final Event Ready is still required after voting; a vote timer cannot start
  unready clients or overwrite an unsaved garage draft.

Online/Offline and friends:
- With network disabled from process start, reach Offline Play, save/reload a
  local campaign, customize cars, complete a local event, and use the offline
  meet/boombox. No external stack, login or fake online friend is required.
- Go Online authenticates and loads the actual online profile; failure leaves
  local data intact. No local balance/entitlement/RP/record is accepted online.
- Case-insensitive handle uniqueness is atomic. Friend-request retries don't
  duplicate edges. Public card lookup exposes no email or credentials.
- Friends panel is on-demand, keyboard/controller usable and unavailable Offline.
  Stale status, invitation denial, full convoy and permission change are visible.
- Password policy is actually enforced through supported provider paths, including
  resets and Unicode/byte-limit cases; logs and error responses reveal no secrets.

Rejoin:
- Confirmed disconnect removes active membership/readiness/ballot but stores the
  correct grant. Brief network jitter doesn't instantly DQ someone.
- Valid rejoin with same epoch works; Not Now preserves eligibility without
  popup spam. Full convoy, changed leader, disband, kick/block, joining another
  convoy and expired transport token all yield the specified distinct behavior.
- Leadership returning to the same account later does not revive an old epoch.
  No reserved phantom seat; no seventh human; no DQ entrant resumes driving.
- A finished driver's durable payout survives disconnect and is not paid twice.

Audio and presentation:
- Opening title and post-login menus use distinct tracks. Garage mix is calmer.
  Every special cue is excluded from generic menus/Freeplay until only its
  permitted encounter or unlocked boombox context applies.
- OST acquisition has a valid source event and grants exactly once. Hearing a
  friend's cue or buying its course does not unlock it. Local unlocks stay local.
- Two users contend for boombox control, queue requests, leave/rejoin and late-
  join playback without overlap, rapid spam or duplicated AudioSources. Verify
  local mute/spoiler protection cannot stop playback for other listeners.
- R40/R48 are rejected as Freeplay, Cup, friendly-AI and ordinary replay targets
  by server code, not only omitted from portraits. Campaign replay still works.
- Capture real close-up vehicle/customization evidence and performance at the
  largest allowed grid. No visible damage appears after repeated hard contacts,
  on replay, in results, or at the meet. Cosmetic parts replicate consistently.

Evidence and delivery:
- Run actual compiled clients and servers; update real schemas/tests/assets.
  A page of records, authored catalogue, or simulation count is not completion.
- Test network impairment and at least one authorized external-network scenario
  as in the master. Distinguish automated driving, human feel tests, measured
  performance, unavailable hardware, blocked external services and unrun gates.
- Record source/build/ruleset versions and actual results. Preserve useful failing
  tests. Do not delete assertions or mislabel evidence from an old six-car build.
- Finish with source/assets, playable builds, current run instructions, original
  and new content coverage, selected implementation screenshots/clips, actual
  test results, verified remote branch/commit and LFS recovery evidence, and an
  honest limitations report. Do not claim art quality or fun from a unit test.
