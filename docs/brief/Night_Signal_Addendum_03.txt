0.1 WHAT THIS REVISION CHANGES

The master already requires mph/km/h selection, chase/hood/bumper cameras,
camera collision and comfort options, real elevation/banking/crests, course
checkpoints, and a +3-second last-safe-checkpoint recovery. Preserve them.

However, its cockpit is explicitly optional. It does not mandate a selectable
circular speedometer, two distinct chase distances, or the detailed drift-
responsive framing and perimeter speed-line treatment requested here.

This revision therefore:
- Requires TWO speedometer styles with real calibrated values: Instrument Dial
  and Digital Strip. Both support mph and km/h.
- Makes FIVE driving views mandatory on every playable full-size car: Chase
  Close, Chase Far, Hood, Bumper / Road, and genuine Cockpit. This explicitly
  replaces the master's optional-cockpit clause and interior-silhouette-only
  sufficiency for the player's driving view.
- Defines responsive arcade motion as a tuned presentation system, with full
  user control and a stable comfort alternative, not random continuous shake.
- Strengthens real route elevation, finite 3D checkpoint validation, circuit
  lap rules, and safe recovery, particularly on overlapping mountain roads.
- Applies the same driving-view/HUD choices to the Garage Test Yard, without
  changing its no-reward/private-practice boundary or A/B comparison rules.

No additional full-size course count is introduced. Improve the required
courses rather than adding placeholders to the catalogue. Temporary flat
engineering fixtures can exist but cannot be labeled finished race courses.

1. CONFIGURABLE INSTRUMENT CLUSTER

1.1 Two complete, visibly distinct styles

Expose Settings -> Driving Display -> Speedometer Style, with live previews:

INSTRUMENT DIAL
A legible round analog-style speedometer face, roughly a 240–270-degree needle
sweep, measured major/minor marks, readable numerals, a visible unit label,
and a prominent digital speed value. It should look designed for the Signal /
Sector arcade identity: deliberate spacing, contrast, clean markings and a
controlled accent, not a generic progress ring with numbers pasted around it.

The dial measures VEHICLE SPEED. A tachometer may sit alongside or around it,
but must be labeled RPM and visually distinct. Gear, RPM/shift guidance and
speed cannot all become an ambiguous red circle. A high-speed mark is not an
engine redline. The two instruments must respond to their actual different
signals, including wheelspin, a gearshift and coasting in neutral.

DIGITAL STRIP
Retain and refine the compact horizontal speed presentation the user has seen,
with a prominent numerical value, unit label and meaningful scale/fill. Keep
gear/RPM information separately identifiable. Do not delete the original
compact preference merely because the new dial is more ornate.

Both styles carry the same essential driving information. Support high contrast,
HUD scaling and safe margins. Keep the same predictable area of the HUD across
styles so switching does not relocate every unrelated race indicator. Do not
cover the apex, opponents, minimap, finish information or contact warnings.

1.2 Units and measured scale

Expose Speed Units: km/h and mph. These correspond to the user's KMPH/MPH
request; use the normal short labels in the finished interface.

Use one canonical physical speed value, then convert only for display:
  km/h = speed_metres_per_second * 3.6
  mph  = speed_metres_per_second / 0.44704

The simulation, event thresholds, part parameters and timing remain in their
existing canonical units. Unit selection must not change a speed-dependent
assist, drift threshold, camera-effect threshold, braking distance, AI pace,
reward, benchmark, or race eligibility. Camera thresholds should consume the
physical value, never the currently displayed integer.

Share the conversion/formatting logic across driving HUD, genuine dashboard
instruments where unit switching is supported, Garage measurements, speed-based
objective text, comparisons, results and spectating. Preserve metric source
units in debugging evidence and identify them clearly. Do not label unchanged
km/h numbers as mph or convert an already-converted value a second time.

Major tick labels, minor intervals and needle/fill position must all correspond
to the chosen scale. Choose uncluttered round intervals for that scale. It is
not necessary for an mph dial and km/h dial to have the same numeric maximum.
Document the mapping so a needle over 60 mph means the same physical speed as
96.56064 km/h, not the same fraction of two unrelated undocumented ranges.

Select a suitable readable range from the car's supported speed envelope and
headroom when the car/build is selected, not by recalculating max scale every
frame. Keep it stable during a run. A faster downhill excursion must not wrap
the needle or hide the true numerical value: show a bounded over-range state
when needed and revise an inadequate range at a safe boundary. Never clamp the
vehicle itself to the dial's range. No fictitious top-speed promise is created
by printing a large number on an instrument.

1.3 Telemetry source and smoothing

Use the game's real chassis-speed telemetry, not throttle percentage, a canned
animation, or wheel angular speed alone. Clarify the physical speed definition
in shared code. For ordinary supported road motion, include longitudinal and
lateral travel along the current road surface so that a sideways drift or a
steep climb does not falsely read almost zero or lose its slope component.

Keep a stable support-surface reference and a defined airborne fallback. A
wheel spinning while stationary cannot show the car traveling at 200 km/h;
falling vertically into a void cannot produce a rewarded road-speed record.
Short legal crest airtime must not flicker the display to zero. Presentation
must not rewrite authoritative movement or scoring to hide a bad measurement.
If a telemetry correction changes metric semantics, version/test that change
explicitly instead of silently comparing old and new records.

For the local driver use the valid predicted/interpolated driving state, with
normal authoritative correction; for spectating use the selected car's valid
replicated state. Follow a consistent sampling/update path for dial and digits.
A small, frame-rate-independent needle filter is appropriate; the number must
remain responsive. Start with roughly 60–120 ms visual smoothing and tune it.
Do not conceal sustained speed loss or invent spring overshoot in the digits.
Reset the filter on teleport, car switch and target destruction.

For reverse motion show speed magnitude with the actual reverse gear indicator;
do not display a negative physical speed as a needle spinning below zero. Define
behavior at a standstill, disconnect, missing target and non-finite telemetry.
Show a neutral unavailable state when appropriate, never a stale stranger's car.

1.4 Preferences and preview

Store local presentation preferences with schema defaults and migration. Online
sign-in, going offline, changing cars, respawning, returning from a toy or ending
a cinematic must not reset them. Do not overwrite another local user's settings.
Do not force preferences onto peers or put them into economic build hashes.

Allow a clearly labeled simulated instrument preview in Settings. A preview is
not a live dyno reading or test result. In the Test Yard, show actual telemetry.
Apply style/units changes without a scene reload, race restart, input loss,
convoy vote reset or unintended throttle input. Preserve existing user choices
when new preferences are added.

2. FIVE MANDATORY DRIVING CAMERA VIEWS

Expose Settings -> Camera -> Driving View and retain a remappable Cycle Camera
action. Keep the default C binding unless the user already remapped it. Controller
cycling must also be available and documented. Keyboard/controller focus in a
text field, menu or shared drawing tool must not switch a race camera accidentally.

CAMERA VIEW CONTRACTS

CHASE CLOSE
A low-to-medium-height, close trailing camera that keeps the whole useful car
silhouette and enough road ahead visible. The player should see their livery,
stance and drift angle without the car filling half the view. It is the primary
arcade tracking view described in section 3.

CHASE FAR
A materially farther/higher trailing view with more road context. It is a
separate calibrated anchor/distance, not the Close view with a misleading label
or a tiny FOV adjustment. It retains an engaging car presence, safe occlusion
and the same optional arcade motion language at appropriately scaled strength.
The user's 'Drone' means this tethered following camera, not a freely flying
reconnaissance tool that can inspect the whole track during a race.

HOOD
A body-mounted viewpoint over the bonnet, with the actual bonnet visible and a
clear road view. Fit the anchor per chassis, including long/short bonnet shapes
and supported appearance changes. It is not interchangeable with Bumper.

BUMPER / ROAD
A low forward viewpoint near the front of the car with a largely unobstructed
road view. Keep it above the road and outside obstructing own-body geometry at
normal grades/crests. Do not place it under the chassis or beyond walls. Give
this view conservative motion so the road remains readable near ground level.

COCKPIT
A true 3D seated-driver view inside the selected car. Show a fitted windshield
opening, dashboard, steering wheel and relevant near-cabin framing. Respect
left/right driving position as authored for that fictional model. The visible
wheel turns coherently with steering; gauges that are readable as functional
instruments must use actual telemetry. Maintain usable night lighting and clear
glass without making a pitch-black dashboard or giant opaque reflections.

All five views are required across all 18 cars and their supported visual
configurations. Reuse rig/controller code and modular cabin parts, not an
identical misplaced cabin intersecting every chassis. Fit seat/eye, dashboard,
wheel, hood and bumper anchors in model-local data. Check roofs, wings, mirrors,
ride-height extremes, body kits, tinted windows and different car proportions.
No camera choice may silently become Hood because Cockpit is not finished.

Visible cockpit detail is mandatory; exhaustive back-seat interiors, functioning
infotainment, dozens of animated dashboard switches, real-time mirror cameras,
head tracking and VR are not newly required. Existing driver rigs may supply
hands/arms where they render correctly; do not show a floating head, duplicate
body or severely clipping hands in lieu of a clean cockpit. Close-up cabin asset
quality must match the rest of the car, not a low-resolution placeholder overlay.

2.1 Calibration, cycling and temporary overrides

Store calibrated anchors/framing per car model. Global view preference and
comfort settings remain separate from mechanical loadouts. A conservative seat
height/fore-aft adjustment for Cockpit is allowed within the physical cabin;
it must not let the camera leave the car or look through its roof.

Support look-back with clear hold/release behavior and consistent return to the
selected driving view. If a rear cabin is not authored, use a clearly presented
external rear-view look-back rather than displaying broken interior geometry.
Spectators choose their own supported view and target without changing the
observed driver's settings. Losing a target selects a valid replacement or a
clear waiting view. No spectator camera input controls any car.

Switch views promptly with a short occlusion-safe blend, or a clean cut/fade
when blending would pass through a roof, wall or car. Preserve the last view
across race intro, skipped cutscene, results, reconnect/spectate, respawn and
Garage return. A scripted shot is a temporary override, not a preference edit.
Repeated cycling cannot accumulate active cameras, listeners or render textures.

Only the relevant camera should perform the expensive render. Additional rigs
can be data/inactive virtual views. A six-player session does not require every
client to render thirty live cameras, and the headless server needs no cameras
or postprocessing. No split-screen requirement is added.

3. ARCADE CAMERA TRACKING: RESPONSIVE, COMPOSED, ADJUSTABLE

The target is a car that feels attached to an energetic camera operator, not a
rigid Transform offset and not uncontrolled cinematic wobble. Movement follows
what the vehicle is actually doing. It should make a drift easier to read while
preserving the driver's ability to judge position, speed, braking and exits.

3.1 Stable base tracking

Track the visual/predicted chassis reference at a consistent stage of the frame,
not noisy wheel meshes or alternating unsmoothed server/physics transforms.
Choose frame-rate-independent damping and a compatible interpolation/update
order. Avoid applying the same correction independently in several rigs.

Maintain the intended trailing distance and screen-space framing with a bounded
spring/damper response. Small responsive movement under acceleration/braking,
load transfer, road compression and a landing is appropriate. At a parked idle
or constant-speed smooth straight there should be no arbitrary pendulum motion.
A little settling is personality; permanent camera wobble is not.

Use a filtered road/travel direction and vehicle orientation to frame the
upcoming visible line. Look-ahead is bounded by nearby route context and real
motion. It must not force human steering, reveal an invisible racing line, see
through a barrier, or rotate wildly toward a spatially close but unrelated
switchback. Smoothly handle road grade without snapping to every surface normal.
Do not lock all pitch to world horizontal and leave a steep descent offscreen.

3.2 Drift entry, sustained drift and exit

Determine drift presentation from valid speed, road/contact state, signed slip,
yaw/turn state and meaningful duration, with entry/exit hysteresis. Countersteer
can point opposite to the corner; a raw steering button alone is not the drift
side. A stationary steering wiggle must not alternate dramatic camera sweeps.

For Chase Close/Far:
- As a meaningful drift begins, ease toward a modest lateral offset and yaw
  framing that reveals the car's side and the approaching path/apex.
- Keep the car anchored within the safe framing region and retain a useful road
  view. Show enough of the vehicle to appreciate the angle without orbiting to
  a side-on cinematic that hides the exit.
- During a sustained drift, follow its stable direction without switching sides
  on small oscillations. Scale the treatment gently with physical speed and
  meaningful slip, not with purchased score boosts.
- On linked left/right transitions, pass smoothly through neutral. Suppress
  rapid sign chatter. Recover toward ordinary chase framing when grip returns.
- Reduce decorative tracking when reversing, stationary, spinning out, airborne,
  in a recovery transition, or suffering large network correction. A spin does
  not authorize a full camera orbit. Keep controls understandable.

Initial tuning envelope for an ordinary approximately 1.8-metre-wide car:
- Lateral drift displacement up to roughly 0.35–0.65 metres at normal intensity.
- Additional drift yaw around 6–12 degrees, not a compulsory large rotation.
- Entry settling around 0.12–0.22 s; release around 0.25–0.45 s.
- Bounded look-ahead around 0.2–0.5 s of travel, subject to road visibility and
  framing. These are design starting points, not a certified handling model.

Calibrate with both drift directions, different wheelbases and tight mountain
walls. Reduce displacement where collision/framing requires it. If a chosen side
would hide the road or place the camera inside a cliff, prioritize visibility
and safety over preserving an ornamental offset. Do not alternate sides based
on frame-to-frame occlusion noise.

Cockpit/Hood/Bumper keep their physical viewpoint identity. Give them restrained
load-transfer/head motion rather than moving a cockpit camera outside a window
to imitate the chase effect. They can share optional peripheral effects, but
must remain usable with a completely stable presentation.

3.3 Acceleration, braking, impacts and speed-FOV

Use small directed, decaying responses to actual local-car events. An impact
can produce a capped short impulse; it must not retrigger every physics tick
or stack indefinitely against a wall. A remote car bump, a toy collision or a
shared-room emote must not shake all drivers' race cameras. Distinguish normal
presentation from a network correction and avoid counting a correction twice.
No impact effect adds visible/mechanical damage or secretly changes handling.

Optional speed-dependent FOV should widen subtly and recover smoothly. Start
with a maximum roughly 4–7 degrees of added VERTICAL FOV at normal intensity,
subject to the player's preferred field of view and comfort. Zero strength means
no dynamic FOV. Keep the base FOV slider labeled consistently as vertical or
horizontal; do not mix definitions between cameras or aspect ratios. Validate
ultrawide framing, not just 16:9. No compulsory blur, fisheye road warp, sudden
zoom punch, or false speed reading is permitted.

3.4 Camera collision and transitions

Resolve occlusion with the car body, ground, guardrails, cliffs, tunnel walls,
bridge structures and nearby scenery using appropriate collision filtering.
Test the camera's near-plane volume, not just a point that leaves half the
screen inside a wall. The local car may be ignored for an exterior visibility
cast, but that is not permission for a cockpit eye to leave the cabin.

Compress distance and damp recovery where necessary. Prefer a safe near framing
to clipping; restore the selected distance without pumping/jittering repeatedly.
Do not globally hide opponent cars or make all walls transparent to solve camera
placement. Visual-only local occlusion treatments, if needed, must be bounded,
clearly scoped and must not reveal another track layer through the mountain.

On respawn/teleport, notify/reset the camera's tracking history and velocities.
Use an appropriate cut/fade to the new valid pose. Never interpolate the view
through hundreds of metres of rock or keep an old drift offset on the new spawn.
Leaving a cinematic, changing car/view and selecting a spectator target require
the same deliberate cleanup. A stuck camera is not a reason to teleport the car.

4. STYLIZED PERIMETER SPEED LINES AND COMFORT CONTROLS

Add an original lightweight graphic treatment around the viewport edge: narrow
streaks or directional accents suggesting speed. It should suit Signal / Sector,
not copy franchise frames. At speed in a committed drift, strength rises modestly
above the ordinary straight-line speed treatment and recedes smoothly afterward.

Keep the central driving view clear: target at least the central 70% of the
viewport's width AND height free of streaks, with the remaining effect confined
to a feathered outer band. Mask beneath the HUD and never cover time, speed,
checkpoint warnings, readiness or subtitles. Do not use an opaque dark vignette
that conceals the verge. Do not strobe, flash a full-screen color, or create a
continuous tunnel of lines at low speed.

Strength is based on real speed and qualified drift state. Wheelspin at a stop,
falling, being towed by a reset, slow donuts or a score multiplier cannot create
a high-speed presentation. Suspend it in paused/menu presentation and transition
cleanly through replay, Test Yard, spectator and race contexts. Keep the element
unscaled by the camera's physical impulse so the instrument HUD remains stable.

Settings must expose:
- Driving View and separate base FOV; Cycle Camera and Look Back bindings.
- Motion preset: Arcade / Comfort / Custom.
- Drift framing strength (including zero).
- Body/road motion strength (including zero).
- Impact shake strength (including zero).
- Decorative camera roll strength (including zero).
- Dynamic speed-FOV strength (including zero).
- Speed lines: Off / Subtle / Strong, or a clearly equivalent labeled slider.
- Existing motion blur toggle, off by default, and applicable HUD size/contrast.

Arcade defaults should demonstrate the requested style without maximal effects:
moderate drift framing, light road/impact response, minimal decorative roll,
subtle speed-FOV and Subtle lines. Comfort uses stable follow/road visibility,
no decorative bob/roll/impact shake, no speed-FOV and no lines. Essential camera
collision, following and view switching remain functional in every preset.

Honor an existing Reduced Motion setting; adding this feature cannot re-enable
excluded motion. Provide a reversible profile selection/preview and store custom
values so disabling effects does not erase them. New defaults are not permission
to silently overwrite existing explicit preferences. Preserve keyboard/controller
access and text readability. These controls change presentation, not race assists
or eligibility, and do not invalidate somebody else's readiness.

There is no universally verified comfortable setting. Record actual playtest
feedback separately from the fact that the sliders work. Do not claim motion-
sickness prevention or broad accessibility validation from a screenshot.

5. REAL ROAD HEIGHT AND TOPOLOGY

5.1 Elevation affects the drivable surface

Build route centreline, road mesh/colliders, terrain joins, barriers, AI planning,
checkpoint volumes and recovery anchors in the same 3D coordinate system. The
road itself rises, falls, cambers and banks. A mountain backdrop behind a flat
road, cosmetic rumble texture, tilted entire level, or tiny random vertex noise
is not completion of this requirement.

Downhill touge routes must produce a sustained, intentional descent with distinct
upper/middle/lower stretches, linked switchbacks, sightline changes, compressions
and exit preparation appropriate to their authored briefs. Allow naturally placed
flat staging areas and small counter-slopes; do not force a mathematically
monotonic descent at every vertex or turn the road into a rollercoaster.

Preserve the existing course identities and variety. Appendix A already contains
uphill and mixed-elevation routes as well as descents. Do not convert C02 or a
climbing-ratio course into a downhill course just because many touge events are
descents. A new downhill variant, if ever separately approved, is not a new base
course for counts. The existing catalogue remains the authoring reference.

Circuits return to their start height but need not be flat. Use real undulations,
bridge approaches, berms or banking suited to their location, and connect the
last segment continuously to the first in position and orientation. A simple
oval can have banked turns and a measured grade/crest without requiring invented
mountain hairpins. A dock or airfield course should retain its believable setting
rather than acquire arbitrary huge hills; modest engineered relief and banking
are appropriate. Preserve all 29 required full-size course identities.

The meet's flat walking/parking apron, instrument tests, dyno, precision braking
lane and skid pad are intentional exceptions. Do not destabilize those facilities
merely to give every square metre a nonzero slope. The Test Yard's connected loop
still includes the elevation/camber element required in Addendum 02.

5.2 Physical coherence and measurement

Use smooth joins and sensible changes of gradient/curvature. Suspensions/contact
queries, gravity, grip and load transfer must respond to the real surface, using
the same rules for players, friendly AI and opposing AI. No invisible force that
keeps AI glued to a crest that launches equivalent human cars. Tune high-speed
crests/compressions to avoid unavoidable crashes, bottoming or tunneling with
legal builds. Do not add hidden steering/autopilot to paper over bad geometry.

Generate a per-course profile report with actual driven-route length, start/end
height, minimum/maximum height, accumulated ascent/descent, grade and banking
ranges, and a sampled elevation trace. For loops report range and ascent/descent;
net elevation zero does not demonstrate flatness. Compare measurements with the
original authoring targets and document deliberate adjustments. Do not present
a typed target from the catalogue as a measurement of the mesh.

Use the actual travelled centreline length in 3D for topology and progress tools,
not only its top-down projection. Road ribbon, walls, guardrails, support terrain,
checkpoints, spawn points, camera blockers, minimap and AI route must agree after
editing. Avoid gaps at LOD/streaming/mesh seams and validate under the longest,
lowest and fastest supported car configurations.

Profile the real twelve-vehicle case. Load/cook required collision before claiming
Main Load Ready. Missing collision during a streaming failure is an error to handle,
not a deliberate trap, normal driver failure, or an opportunity to invent results.

6. CHECKPOINTS, LAP PROGRESSION AND ANTI-SHORTCUT VALIDATION

6.1 Separate sectors, checkpoints and recovery anchors

A sector is a timing/reporting split. A checkpoint is a required progress gate.
A recovery anchor is a safe placement associated with legal progress. They can
share an authored location, but they are different concepts. Having three timing
sectors does not prove there are enough checkpoints to prevent a hairpin shortcut.

Every full-size event needs intermediate checkpoint coverage between start and
finish, placed around shortcut-risk areas, stacked roads, overpasses, long bends,
course branches and meaningful transitions. Add sufficient gates/continuity zones
for the actual geometry; do not put exactly three giant trigger slabs on every
map and call it valid. Road signs/arches/reflectors can show important gates,
with an optional next-checkpoint cue and readable missed-route warning.

Each event route definition contains stable ordered gate IDs, lap/route direction,
finite 3D gate bounds, forward crossing normal, legal connected approach/departure
segments, road-layer identity where needed, sector mapping and safe anchors.
Reverse variants need their own ordered orientation/anchor interpretation.

6.2 Accept only legal ordered traversal

The authority advances the expected gate only for a valid forward crossing of its
finite bounds with legal route continuity and plausible motion. Sweep the actual
movement interval so high-speed crossings are not lost between frames. Use route-
appropriate tolerances for contact/jitter and legitimate short crest airtime.
Never create an infinitely tall gate that a car falling down the mountain can
trip, or use a client-reported checkpoint/finish index as authoritative truth.

Track the player's current legal segment/topological neighborhood. In a stack of
switchbacks, an adjacent section in X/Z or even Euclidean distance is not
necessarily the next section of the route. A bridge deck and the road beneath it
are different surfaces. Confirm the route and height context rather than simply
choosing the nearest spline point. Legitimate connectors are authored explicitly.

Missing or bypassing a gate does not advance progress. Falling onto a lower,
later road cannot grant intermediate gates, valid sectors, clean bonuses,
benchmark success or a finish. Returning to the correct unfinished section can
restore a legal route; do not force a restart of the entire event for a repairable
mistake. Repeated reverse/forward movement across a gate is idempotent and cannot
award its score, time bonus or checkpoint count repeatedly.

Differentiate short permitted jumps/shoulder excursions from leaving the legal
route volume. Not every millisecond without four contacts is a cheat or reset.
But a landed car on an unrelated road section remains off-route even though it
has a ground collider under it. Standing on terrain under a bridge is not a
legal substitute for driving across the bridge.

Use shared rules for human and AI progress. Debug teleportation, recovery moves,
spawn initialization and replay playback are marked discontinuities, not ordinary
swept driving segments. A teleport must not cross/award every gate on its path.
Replay visuals have no authority to collide with triggers or produce rewards.

6.3 Circuits and legitimate finishes

For a circuit, store current lap and the expected gate in that lap. Arm the
start/finish crossing only after every mandatory gate for that lap has been
accepted in order. Use a consistent initial-grid state so the launch across the
line does not count as completing lap one. Backing over the line, oscillating
around it, entering it from another road layer or jumping into its volume cannot
complete a lap. A valid finish advances exactly once at the prescribed final lap.

Preserve prior completed laps during recovery. A fallback behind an earlier gate
of the current lap must update that lap's cursor consistently rather than keep
incompatible progress or delete already completed full laps. All clients agree
on lap, ordered progress, position, penalties and finish; server timing and
precision/tie rules remain authoritative.

Rank racers using legal lap/gate progress plus bounded progress on the currently
permitted segment, not nearest geometric distance to the finish. A fallen car
may lose position; it must not jump to first because it is physically near the
bottom of the mountain. Keep provisional UI interpolation separate from verified
results. Timing, source version and discontinuities belong in replay evidence.

7. SAFE COURSE RECOVERY

7.1 Offer a prompt before a mistake becomes a long wait

Keep the remappable hold-to-reset action, initially R on keyboard. A starting
hold duration of roughly 0.75 seconds is suitable; expose clear progress and
cancel-on-release, plus an accessible confirmation alternative where appropriate.
Holding the key after a completed reset cannot trigger repeated resets: require
release/rearm or a new distinct request. Typing in menus or the Canvas cannot
reset an unrelated racing/test car.

Prompt promptly for overturned, stuck or off-route vehicles. Use authored bounds,
road-layer state, orientation, support and travel progress, not one global world-Y
threshold. A deep valid tunnel is not below-world. A car falling onto a lower
switchback is not safely on route. Short recoverable kerb/grass excursions should
not trigger a punitive automatic teleport.

Provide automatic rescue for an unambiguous void/fall or prolonged unrecoverable
state, with a visible brief notice/countdown where practical. Initial target:
resolve an unmistakable dangerous fall within about 2–4 seconds of confirmed
loss of the legal route, not a ten-second fall to a distant kill plane. Tune
thresholds by route and actual evidence, preserving legitimate crest behavior.
Manual and automatic recovery share the same authority and no-advantage policy.

7.2 Choose a legal safe destination, not the closest road

The server selects a safe anchor at or behind the last accepted checkpoint in
the current route/lap, associated with the legal approach already driven. Before
the first checkpoint use a safe route-start anchor. Never move the car forward
to the spatially closest road, a later checkpoint, a different layer or a finish.

An anchor includes position/orientation fitted to the drivable surface and valid
collision clearance for the actual car footprint/ride height. Provide alternative
nearby placements for occupancy, not an unrestricted nearest-road search. Face
the event's forward direction, reset inappropriate linear/angular motion and
wheel/contact transients, and keep the current validated build. Recovery does not
swap loadouts or repair damage because damage is not part of this game.

If the latest anchor is obstructed or invalid, select a validated earlier anchor
in the same lap or its safe start. Reconcile the current-lap checkpoint cursor
with any backward fallback; never preserve impossible forward segment credit.
No fallback may remove already completed full laps. Avoid repeatedly placing the
car inside the same wall, another vehicle, below the mesh or outside the corridor.

7.3 Atomic recovery and its consequences

Each completed recovery is one idempotent authoritative event with a request/nonce,
actor/event ID, destination, current-lap progress, penalty, score effects, server
tick and reason. Duplicate requests/responses have one effect. A UI timeout cannot
silently apply two penalties or spawn two cars. Do not penalize a reset which
failed before a valid destination/action was established.

During a competitive event, keep the existing +3.000-second penalty once per
completed recovery and keep the race clock running. Show elapsed/raw time and
penalties/adjusted time consistently; do not count the three seconds twice by
both sleeping the clock and adding the same numerical penalty. Recovery clears
the unbanked drift chain and invalidates relevant clean-run/no-reset goals,
without erasing legitimately banked score or unrelated permanent progression.
No immediate DQ, wallet charge, RP removal or race restart is introduced.

Moving reset protection lasts at most two seconds and is car-to-car only. It
cannot bypass road barriers or be chained into an overtaking advantage. Check
occupancy before release. If safe contact cannot be restored within that window,
hold the car at a validated non-progressing protected placement or choose another
safe non-forward anchor; do not extend moving ghost mode indefinitely or force
solid overlap with another car. The one recovery still incurs one penalty.
Time Attack's already non-contact vehicle policy remains separate.

Settle server errors separately from ordinary driver mistakes. Missing collision,
invalid authoring data or a server abort must be logged and handled without
inventing a player's legal finish or treating every affected player as a quitter.
Keep existing durable finish/transaction rules. This does not authorize a new
blanket compensation economy or client-submitted rewards.

Online clients request recovery; they cannot supply their own trusted destination,
checkpoint, lap or penalty. Apply the normal prediction/correction/teleport
notification to remote views. Reset camera smoothing, effects and telemetry on
all relevant clients. A disconnected DQ entrant cannot regain control through
Reset, a view change, rejoin, a loadout preset or the private Test Yard.

Tutorial/private Garage testing can reset freely according to its practice rules,
without competitive penalties, currency, stage clears, mastery or official ghost
records. The miniature Pocket Circuit keeps its own slot-car recovery/laps and
is never counted as full-size driving evidence. Do not apply a toy's permissive
reset semantics to the main races.

8. INTEGRATION, MIGRATION AND PERFORMANCE

8.1 Use existing systems deliberately

Reuse the current presentation framework, vehicle controller and route tools.
Cinemachine or a tested equivalent can supply damping/occlusion/impulses; no new
package or global version migration is automatically required. Inspect the
installed package's documentation before using APIs. Do not layer multiple
independent follow/shake scripts that fight over the same camera each frame.

Keep client presentation data distinct from authoritative state. Examples of
useful concepts, not a demand for a new generic framework:
- DrivingDisplayPreferences: style, units, scale/contrast and schema version.
- DrivingCameraPreferences: view, FOV, motion preset and individual strengths.
- CarCameraAnchors: per-model eye/hood/bumper/chase fitting and bounds.
- SpeedTelemetry: canonical units, source/context and valid/airborne state.
- RouteGate/RouteSegment/RecoveryAnchor: finite geometry, connectivity and IDs.
- RaceProgress: current lap, expected gate, valid segment and finish-armed state.
- RecoveryEvent: unique identity, authority tick, destination and consequences.
- CourseProfileReport: measured 3D geometry and rendered/physical agreement.

Keep published shared definitions identical on client/server for progress rules.
Version changed course geometry, gate layouts, physics metrics and replay/record
compatibility. Archive incompatible old records with an explanation; do not
silently compare them against a changed descent or wipe player-owned cars and
campaign clears. Gauge/camera cosmetic preferences by themselves do not make an
old legal driving record incompatible. Pending old-version event settlement uses
its original valid rule version, not a reinterpretation under today's meshes.

8.2 The Test Yard and preferences

All five full-size camera views and both HUD styles work in Test Current, Test
Candidate and Test Ready Build, using the same camera behavior as the main game.
No exclusive 'good showroom camera' which hides the real racing view's problems.
Persist preferences when returning to Garage and entering the agreed event.

Changing only a view, units or motion option does not spend money, apply parts,
change A/B candidates, overwrite protected loadout references, invalidate readiness
or interrupt another participant. Record camera/FOV context in A/B observations
so subjective comparisons remain understandable, but do not claim a settings
change rewrote the car's measured physical performance.

Changing performance candidates still follows Addendum 02's deliberate Unready
rule. Read-only testing of the accepted build can be preempted for the main event
without another lap/parking requirement. Destroy the private car/camera cleanly;
it cannot continue receiving inputs underneath the race or become a second online
entrant. Shared toy boards and session state survive as already specified.

8.3 Resource and update budget

Build/cache dial geometry and tick labels on scale/style/unit changes, not by
allocating a new UI hierarchy every frame. Pool perimeter effects; no large
uncapped particle system or fullscreen expensive blur for a simple line effect.
Avoid recurring managed allocations in the driving/camera loop. Validate frame
pacing with both gauges, every view and the twelve-car network scene.

Render cockpit detail locally at appropriate quality; do not demand high-detail
interiors for every distant opponent. Preserve correct own-car culling and LOD
selection when switching from exterior to cockpit. A missing cabin caused by
aggressive exterior culling is a defect, not a fallback to Bumper.

Measure actual CPU/GPU frame timing, memory and repeated view/scene changes on
named hardware/build settings. Test at more than one frame rate. No universal
performance or human comfort claim follows from a single Editor screenshot.
