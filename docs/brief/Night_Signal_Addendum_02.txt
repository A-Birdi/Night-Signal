while someone tunes. Test the candidate car. Rejoin the next event without

user. It is not a new project, optional idea list, permission to rebuild from
scratch, or a replacement for completing the main multiplayer racing game.

1. THE SHARED DOWNTIME FEATURE: 'WHILE WE WAIT'

1.1 Availability and presentation

Add one clear 'While We Wait' control to Home, the ordinary Garage menus,
event briefing, campaign/freeplay selection, and the safe post-event flow.
It opens a compact activity selector, not a new matchmaking system. Show the
five toys, who is playing each, and whether their shared state is paused.
Joining does not require every convoy member to participate or become ready.

Support different subsets concurrently: two people at Cap Clash, one assembling
a model, another drawing, and another driving a private Garage test while the
sixth edits a car. Do not make one person's activity choice change everyone
else's screen. A player actively controls one diversion or Test Yard at a time;
other persistent boards remain available without consuming active controls.

At the meet, provide tasteful physical equivalents: a workbench, a start-light
practice device, the tabletop track, and the communal display board. Activating
a prop and opening its menu shortcut lead to the SAME convoy-owned state.
Meeting in the same public instance is not membership in the same toy session.
Non-convoy visitors cannot silently edit a private convoy's drawing or project.

Use an in-game panel/3D tabletop presentation within the existing shell. There
must be no required walk to a station, parking sequence, car swap, public match,
full-size track load, or compulsory instruction cutscene before a quick attempt.
First-use help is concise and dismissible. Controller, keyboard and pointer
routes must all exist. Maintain a visible convoy/readiness strip and Close.

Do not steal the current UI when another member opens a toy. Invites are muted,
optional notices, at most one per sender per 30 seconds; joining remains opt-in.
A garage user can inspect a small session-score notice without losing a draft.
The existing Friends overlay and ready notifications stay independently usable.

At-meet avatar presentation must remain truthful: standing at a tabletop or using
an overlay is not driving a second car. Disable conflicting avatar controls
while the panel owns input, then restore them safely. Never publish private
component selections or an account screen as a rich social activity feed.

1.2 Ready is consent, not an activity lock

Keep separate fields for ActivityIntent, local screen/presence, mode agreement,
event readiness, applied loadout revision, and diversion participation.
Examples of combined display:
- 'Ready for S08 | Pocket Circuit'.
- 'Not ready | Garage — testing a candidate'.
- 'At meet | Cap Clash | Mode ready'.

Entering, closing, changing, or spectating a diversion must NOT clear existing
Mode Ready or Event Ready. A player may explicitly ready through its header if
all ordinary eligibility/loadout checks pass. No toy may mark them ready itself.

Existing rules still apply to real event changes: altered rules/course/roster
invalidate the relevant agreement; applying a performance change invalidates
that player's Event Ready and requires validation of the new build. Audio,
accessibility, toy choices, and drawing changes do not modify the race proposal.

A ready player may use the Test Yard with exactly their approved build in the
read-only mode defined later. Testing or editing a different candidate first
requires deliberately leaving Event Ready. Do not silently test one build while
confirming an unrelated build under the same ready marker.

Activity input counts as real interaction for existing Away detection; toy
animations, synthesized inputs, automatic laps, or network heartbeats do not.
A player who steps away can still unready voluntarily. No essential progression
reward depends on remaining entertained, and quiet waiting is always acceptable.

1.3 Main-event preemption

When a mode transition or race allocation is actually committed with valid
consent, the server suspends all active convoy diversions at an explicit state
revision/tick boundary. Save current boards, tool operations, lap progress,
model assembly, and scores. The primary start must NOT wait for:
- a cap to stop sliding;
- a reaction attempt, lap, task, or drawing to finish;
- a minigame victory/result screen;
- an absent participant's 'exit minigame' acknowledgement.

Show a small 'Paused for the convoy — progress kept' notice. Do not add a second
five-second departure countdown merely for a toy. The main event retains its
normal validated load barrier, intro rules, and synchronized countdown.

Already accepted toy actions before the pause boundary apply once; actions after
it are rejected/deferred explicitly and cannot sneak into the frozen snapshot.
Save position AND velocity for a moving cap or toy car, lap elapsed active time,
completed steps and partial accepted assembly progress, and finalized strokes.
A drawing currently being made is finalized through its last accepted point.
An unsubmitted aiming drag is just a draft and may be restored as a draft.

A transport acknowledgement and durable acceptance are distinct. Persist
accepted discrete changes or a bounded recoverable journal; recover a moving
object from its accepted input plus authoritative state. Do not require a
multi-megabyte screenshot upload or replay serialization on the critical start
path. A failed toy save uses the last verified snapshot and an honest warning;
it must not falsely claim complete preservation or block a healthy race solely
on an optional activity-service fault. Main account/service failures retain
normal race-start safety rules.

No global pause operation may stop the race server, other convoys, menu input,
or the account/control connection. Suspend only the relevant toy simulations.

1.4 Returning and resuming

After an event, restore the prior boards, project and session records. Offer
'Resume' rather than forcing all players back into their last toy. Moving toys
remain frozen until an actual participant explicitly resumes that instance.
A one- to two-second local reorientation cue can precede moving/timing input;
there is no penalty or instant failure before the player has control again.

For reaction cues that have already been revealed, restart that one unfinished
micro-attempt with a new unrevealed cue and do not record a failure. Keep all
completed attempts and cooperative progress. Explain this as an interruption,
not a new game. For Pocket Circuit, preserve the partial lap and annotate a
mandatory-resumed lap when relevant; do not claim it was uninterrupted.

If only one participant closes a moving toy, suspend/park only their controlled
piece where possible. Other participants continue. The shared board/project
never requires everybody to return, and nobody's absent turn blocks it. When
nobody is actively interacting, stop unnecessary simulation and visual work.

After loading is canceled/aborted, allow the prior toy to resume with the same
snapshot. A second start retry must not duplicate a cap, an assembly operation,
a lap completion, a stroke, or a table occupant.

1.5 Lifetime, membership and dormant rooms

Use a stable ConvoySessionId and per-membership generation, separate from the
leader's identity, leadership epoch, active race ID and toy-panel instances.
Keep one saved board/state per authored layout, one active assembly project,
a small completed-project shelf, the Canvas document, and bounded score history.

Individual disconnect:
- Remove active membership/readiness under Addendum 01, and release input/task
  leases. Mark their toy seat dormant rather than erase its records.
- Their accepted art and cooperative work remain. Suspend their slot car and
  keep their last accepted cap position or safe pending-shot state.
- Authorized rejoin to the same convoy restores the dormant toy identity without
  spawning another piece. It does not restore Event Ready or a DQ race entry.

Leadership transfer does not reset the toys. Rejoin grants still obey Addendum
01's leadership-epoch rule: an old grant can become invalid even though the
shared project survives. A fresh authorized invite to the same live session may
restore the person's dormant toy data; possession of data is not authorization.

Closing a panel, switching toy, spectating, moving to Garage, a race transition,
or being temporarily disconnected is NOT an explicit departure from convoy.

Explicit Leave/Forget Convoy, a kick, joining another convoy, or account
revocation ends that membership's active toy participation. Remove/retire its
physical cap or slot car at a safe revision, release leases, and remove queued
actions. Keep already incorporated drawing/project contributions unless an
explicit supported reset changes them. Do not destructively rewind everyone
else's shared work. A new membership generation does not inherit stale input
rights. Historical attribution is not a persistent account reward.

Last person explicitly leaves, or leader explicitly disbands: terminate the
session, explaining that its shared toys end. Offer ordinary viewing of already
saved personal blueprints when those exist; do not silently export everybody's
artwork or retain private room content forever.

ALL members lost only to network disconnection: instead of immediate disband,
mark the convoy Dormant for at most 24 hours, measured on the server from the
loss of the last active member. Persist compact state and valid rejoin grants;
release running simulation/meet/race resources. No active seat is reserved, no
current race resumes driving, and no server process must run just for the toy.
An explicit rejoin under normal authorization restores the room and starts a
new active period. Background retries or a heartbeat alone do not renew expiry.
Normal disband/revocation/leadership rules still apply. At expiry, invalidate
membership grants and retire session data under the documented retention rule.
This is the sole all-disconnected lifetime override to Addendum 01.

Server restart recovery uses stored authorized state or reports unavailability;
never fabricate a live convoy or restore a canceled race from toy metadata.
Use bounded storage and compact old histories. Preserve current state rather
than dropping active work to keep an unbounded list of old score announcements.

Offline provides the same solo activities under the Local profile, with local
atomic save/recovery, no fake remote players and no online wallet writes. Keep
that personal workspace across offline races and app restarts until reset by its
owner. Do not upload a Local board as authority over an Online convoy on Go Online.

1.6 Economy, accessibility and performance boundaries

All five toys are free to enter and available from initial ordinary menus. Do
not lock them behind campaign completion, OST ownership, a paid car, or a toy
currency. No entry fee, consumables, energy, wager, daily streak, battle pass,
loot boxes, required achievements, or performance-part rewards.

Session scores/crowns, an optional ephemeral nickname, the growing project, and
shared creativity are enough. Toy events cannot satisfy any of the 75 mastery
predicates, pay Credits, grant RP, set records for full-size courses, or unlock
special rivals/OSTs. Use an explicit non-progression activity domain in code.

Use the existing original Signal/Sector UI and sound palette with small tactile
responses. Do not replace the game's soundtrack each time a toy opens. Keep
boombox/menu mixing rules, local mute, motion reduction, readable contrast and
color-independent player identification. No required audio-only timing cue.

Reuse the game's network/session infrastructure and assets sensibly. Do not
install another backend/provider or build a general-purpose minigame platform.
Prioritize race/control traffic over drawing previews and toy snapshots. Cap
message sizes/rates, queued actions, active simulations and memory. Stop hidden
render targets/particles/audio. Report measured warm/cold opening and unload
costs; 'instant' is not a substitute for measurements on the actual build.

2. CAP CLASH — IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

2.1 The toy

An attractive, shallow, tabletop shuffleboard tray sits on a workshop bench.
Caps use small cached livery/color samples from their owners' applied cars,
with readable player marks. Do not change the user's real livery, use a full
2048-pixel texture per cap, or let a paid car improve a cap's physics.

Support one to six humans with one reusable active cap per participant. Each
shot retrieves that player's own cap to a marked launch strip, never somebody
else's. Aim direction and power with pointer drag or an equivalent stick/buttons
control; show an optional guide and power limit. A physics-based, bounded planar
slide with cap-to-cap contact and bank shots is required, not a random result.

Use a maximum one-request-per-person shot queue. Players aim locally without
holding a global lock. Submitting joins a serialized queue; when their turn
arrives, show a brief reconfirm/cancel opportunity if the board changed enough
to invalidate the preview. No response releases the queue within a few seconds.
Bodies settle before the next shot unless a main event pauses them first.
A disconnected, minimized, or closed panel must not hold a shooting lease.

The default is a rolling contest, not a mandatory finite match. A target with
10/25/50 zones makes the current board legible; 'closest cap now' and personal
best shot distance are different metrics. A cap knocked out of the center loses
its current-board crown but not its owner's historical best shot. Exact ties
remain ties within the simulation/display tolerance. Falling off resets that
cap to available-to-shoot, not a long elimination or a lost inventory item.

No countdown forces another person to take a turn. Closing forfeits no currency,
and races may preempt a cap while it is moving. A view-only participant can
watch without joining the shot queue.

2.2 Required content and solo/cooperative value

Provide two saved arrangements:
- 'Workbench Bullseye': broad center target, bumper rails, open risk/reward shots.
- 'Toolbox Banks': intentionally arranged rubberized tool-shaped obstacles with
  at least six authored bank-shot target configurations.

Avoid a scale-accurate tool simulation. The bench should be visually charming,
physically readable, and safe in its fictional setting; its objects are props.

Solo: select reachable targets and improve accuracy/bank-shot consistency.
Cooperative: fill a shared six-mark target card by completing distinct authored
shots; any participant can contribute, including all six marks by one person.
Competitive: compare current positions and session bests. No separate match
allocation is needed when a second player joins. A target card is a checklist,
not a card-game engine. Switching arrangement preserves its previous board and
requires consent from the currently active table users; it never resets another
occupied table behind their back.

Pause snapshots contain cap IDs/owners, transforms, velocities, target/config
revision, queued valid shots, scores and the last accepted command sequence.
Test simultaneous submissions, late spectators, disconnection during a shot,
collision resolution, resume from motion, and no ghost duplicate caps.

3. PIT-CREW PROJECT — IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

3.1 A cooperative thing that grows over the evening

Provide a shared tabletop assembly project using original miniature components.
It must visibly develop with each completed task; this is not a progress bar
filled by repeated button clicks. One to six people can contribute in parallel.
It is a toy project, not maintenance of real cars or a new crafting economy.

Three required blueprints:
- 'Night-Shift Coupe': an original miniature chassis, drivetrain silhouette,
  body panels, cabin, wheels, lights, trim, and final decoration.
- 'Cutaway Four': a fictional display engine showing staged block, crank/piston
  elements, timing marks, pipes/wiring and a clear protective display cover.
- 'Terrace Pit Diorama': a small car-meet/pit display with platform, fence,
  lamps, miniature car, bench and signage/decorative elements.

Each has at least 24 meaningful assembly operations across six recognizable
interaction families: align/fit; rotate/index; controlled tightening; connect a
short route; match timing/alignment marks; place finishing/decorative details.
Use small forgiving sequences, generally 3–15 seconds per operation. Do not
promise mechanical education or real torque specifications from fictional toys.

A failed alignment or mistimed tightening attempts only retries that local
operation. It must not destroy the model, consume currency, undo a friend's
work or require a new item. No mandatory timer, durability, role assignment,
'come back tomorrow', or online friend count to complete the blueprint.

3.2 Parallel work and persistence

Author a task-dependency graph with multiple independent branches, including at
least six start-accessible tasks or subassemblies. Dependencies represent actual
assembly logic, not time gates. Every task is doable by one person; multi-hand
simultaneous button presses are not a requirement for the final steps.

Claiming a task grants a short renewable interaction lease. Renew while actual
interaction is in progress, not just while a window remains open. Idle,
disconnected or preempted contributors release control; accepted partial
progress remains. Another contributor can continue without stealing unsaved
private input. Server orders claims and completion once by operation ID.

Show who is working on which subassembly and readable cursor/tool indicators.
Completed parts are authoritative shared facts; late joiners see the current
model, not replay twenty minutes of animations. No need to network every hand
bone or continuously serialize the full project mesh.

Provide a bounded shelf of at least three finished session models with brief
contribution credits. Nobody can discard an unfinished project with one click.
A destructive reset requires the active contributors' explicit approval and a
recoverable checkpoint. Starting another project after completion preserves the
finished model, subject to an explained shelf limit; no forced reset on racing.

The finished project is not a performance upgrade or an item in the paid garage.
A new blueprint cannot unlock currency, RP, the 75 rewards, or story progress.
Test two contributors finishing the same operation, interrupted progress, full
solo completion, six-person parallel work, and a saved partial model surviving
race and reconnect cycles.

4. GREENLIGHT — IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

4.1 Three immediately understandable variants

'Lights Out': after a randomized unrevealed waiting interval, release/press the
chosen control when the start-light cue changes. Show early/valid/missed states
through shape/text as well as color and sound. No rhythmic fixed delay to memorize.

'Shift Window': use a small stylized rev sweep and the player's configured shift
input to land inside a marked window. Explain early/late and the achieved window;
do not pretend this tiny training sweep measures the torque curve of their car.

'Hold the Mark': hold/release to stop a moving needle near a target. It exercises
controlled timing and can offer forgiving practice or a narrower voluntary
challenge. No excessive flashing, mandatory repetitive mashing, or costly retry.

Each supports individual attempts while showing friends' live attempt indicators
and completed session results. For shared play, members can opt into the same
short cue series, or contribute asynchronously to a convoy chain of clean
attempts. Solo can complete the same chain; inactive members never block it.

4.2 Timing honesty

Separate measured local input timing from network delivery delay. Do not rank
people by the order their packets reached the server. Present times as casual
local timing estimates shared with the convoy; display/input latency can differ.
Use supported high-resolution monotonic timing and actual input edges. Do not
claim medical reaction assessment, tournament fairness, anti-cheat certainty,
or timing resolution beyond the tested input/display path.

The server sanity-checks attempt IDs, variant, legal sequence, plausible timing
ranges, deduplicated submission, and participant identity. There are no economy
consequences and no public ranked leaderboard requiring a new anti-cheat stack.
Display a best and a recent five-attempt median where sufficient results exist;
never invent five samples from one attempt. Unsupported local measurement can
be labeled practice-only rather than produce misleading competitive numbers.

Main-event preemption preserves completed results and chain progress. Restart
an exposed but unfinished cue neutrally after resuming, with a fresh hidden delay.
No failure for closing, pausing, or disconnecting. Do not hold the convoy for the
completion of a five-attempt set. Distinguish genuine voluntary failed attempts
from system interruptions. Provide adjustable cue size/contrast and alternative
inputs without affecting actual driving controls globally.

5. POCKET CIRCUIT — IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

5.1 Mandatory tabletop slot-car play

Implement a real, shared 3D tabletop slot-car toy with visible motion, bend
risk, throttle control, lap timing and slip-off/reset behavior. A prerecorded
animation, decorative track, six bot laps or a menu video does not satisfy it.

Players use analog throttle or a smoothed digital hold. Release to reduce speed
for corners. A small explicit toy model advances along the authored slot and
uses speed/curvature to determine a readable grip threshold. Excessive speed
causes a harmless de-slot and a quick return to the last safe point, not a
catastrophic model breakup or automatic victory/loss of the whole session.

This miniature on-rails model is intentional and isolated. Full-size race cars
remain freely steerable under the existing main vehicle controller. Do not
replace the racing game with slot-car physics or reuse toy lap times as records.

Use miniature representations of the participants' applied cars/liveries where
assets permit, with lightweight LODs. ALL toy cars have equal performance. Real
parts, purchases, campaign ranks, aero kits and account bonuses cannot improve
the toy. Initial generic miniatures are acceptable while the real cars load,
but never a permanent substitute for the recognizable car presentation.

Support one through six independently controlled lanes. Provide local response
with authoritative bounded state correction for Online toy play. Holding input
cannot continue indefinitely after disconnect or focus loss. No split-screen or
extra controllers are required; six genuine clients must be demonstrated.

5.2 Three required authored tabletop layouts

- 'Workshop Oval': fast sweepers and one slow section teaching throttle release.
- 'Coil Pass': a compact sequence of tightening curves and a change of elevation.
- 'Midnight Figure Eight': an over/under crossing, contrasting corner radii and
  a technical run back to the timing gantry.

Aim for roughly 10–25 seconds for a clean lap as a tuning target, not a forced
end to the activity. Each is an actual designed track with clear boundaries,
slot geometry, reset points, camera visibility, audio and a finish gate. They
are not extra full-size courses for the launch minimum.

Equalize lane lengths and curvature demands as far as practical. Record the
actual per-lane differences and validate a common-input/reference driving test.
Raw times are compared only on the same lane/configuration; shared cross-lane
comparison uses a disclosed lane-reference ratio when the geometry differs.
No manufactured claim that an outside slot with a longer path is perfectly
fair. Show both raw lap and normalized comparison, or separate lane bests.
Switching lanes cannot retain an incompatible best as if it were comparable.

5.3 No race-length trap

Default to continuous, drop-in laps and a rolling session best/clean-lap count.
Optional cooperative target: complete a collection of clean laps together.
One player can fill every contribution; no waiting for a particular lane to run.
Optional short head-to-head comparisons must still be interruptible and resumable;
there is no second event queue, mandatory finish line, or compulsory restart.

Only one layout need be actively simulated at a time for the convoy. Store all
three layouts' boards/records so changing layout does not destroy a prior lap.
Current active users explicitly consent to changing the table layout; the main
convoy leader cannot repeatedly interrupt a table just by browsing menus.

Mandatory preemption saves slot position, velocity, active lap timer, lap count,
reset/de-slot markers, control state, participant identity, layout version and
accepted sequence. Resume at the same point after an explicit short orientation
cue. Time spent in the real race, paused, disconnected or reading a resume panel
must not accrue to the toy lap. Restore input safely; do not suddenly apply a
minute-old held throttle before the player regains control.

Voluntary pause/closure midlap preserves the lap for practice but flags that lap
as voluntarily paused, excluding it from uninterrupted session-best categories.
A main-event interruption is labeled separately and cannot erase prior bests.
A de-slotted lap is completed for fun but distinct from a clean-lap record.
No disadvantage is applied to the main racing game for any toy outcome.

Test individual dropout while others keep lapping, full-convoy pause midcorner,
late join, layout change, lane fairness, full solo control, six real clients,
and restoration without duplicated laps or a sudden full-throttle launch.

6. CONVOY CANVAS — IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

6.1 A real shared drawing surface

Provide freehand drawing, not only selecting pre-made sticker buttons. Include
pen/marker, line/shape tools, readable text, color/width, a small original stamp
library, and a way to select/move/rotate/scale one's marks. Use controller cursor
and grid/snapping support alongside pointer drawing. No external image upload,
script execution, real-car-brand library, or new public content marketplace.

The primary surface is a clean whiteboard with readable ink. Provide a second
VIEW of the same artwork on an attractive separate display hood using stable
UV projection. A flat editor remains available; drawing on a curved 3D surface
must not be the only usable method. The preview hood is a toy/display object,
not another member's owned car and not a mechanical performance item.

Make it easy to doodle, write a silly garage slogan, sketch a corner, or combine
stickers without opening the full vehicle-livery workflow. No theme prompt,
judge, vote-to-win, entry fee or drawing ability test is needed.

6.2 Multiplayer editing and recoverability

Assign stable IDs and authorship to strokes/objects, with an authoritative
operation order. Show unobtrusive named cursors. Stream bounded previews and
commit meaningful stroke/transform actions, not whole textures per pointer move.
An operation carries session/document/revision/member identity and a unique ID.
Invalid numbers, excessive points, oversized text and malformed markup are
rejected without crashing another client. Escape all displayed text.

Each participant has at least 50 meaningful own-action Undo/Redo steps. Undoing
one's stroke does not rewind somebody else's later stroke. Own-object erasing is
the default. Editing a friend's object requires an explicit editable/shared
permission, with short leases for transforms to avoid fighting over it.
Covering a drawing with new doodles can be playful; silently deleting all of
another person's work is not an acceptable default permission.

Provide snapshots of the current document at safe intervals and before any
whole-board clear. A full clear/reset requires an explicit proposal accepted by
current active contributors; no countdown treats silence as acceptance. Keep at
least one recoverable pre-clear checkpoint. Returning from a race is never Clear.
Closing, disconnection, and leadership changes retain accepted work.

Start with a compact vector model and a bounded budget (for example a 1 MiB
compressed current document and 4,096 mark objects). Treat these as initial
engineering budgets to measure, not permission to erase unnoticed strokes.
When approaching a limit, compact compatible historical operations, offer a
new sheet while preserving the old one, or explain the limit. Retain at least
three session sheets with clear navigation and bounded revision history.
Compression/decompression and input limits must also bound malicious payloads.

The main-event pause finalizes an in-progress stroke at its last acknowledged
point. Late packets must not paint onto a different sheet or an older epoch.
The hood and whiteboard views render the same committed drawing after rejoin.
An optional explicit 'use as my livery draft' may reuse existing authorized
editor functionality, but is not required for this release and may never apply
art to an owned car, spend money, or overwrite a visual preset automatically.

7. POST-EVENT FLOW: CONTINUE OR SERVICE BREAK

7.1 Resolve the group decision without a majority-rules race start

After a campaign/event becomes terminal and player results are safely recorded,
present an attractive shared decision strip on Results. Do not open a main
continuation vote while somebody is still legitimately racing. A finished
player's earlier safe menu access must not invalidate their earned finish.

Choices:
- 'Next Stage' for a valid forward campaign proposal.
- 'Retry Stage' where the shared progression frontier requires a retry.
- 'Next Event' / 'Return to Event Setup' for Freeplay, Challenges, or a Cup leg.
- 'Garage / Need a Moment' as the service-break choice in every case.

Exactly two principal decisions are shown: Continue to the specified next
step, or Service Break. The Continue label explains the actual step rather
than promising progression the server cannot grant. Keep spectate/review/
leave options accessible as secondary actions where applicable.

Record per-current-member Continue / ServiceBreak / Undecided with source
result ID, roster revision and destination revision. Include the leader.
This is destination consent, NOT consent to an unknown finalized event.

Unanimous Continue enables the leader's explicit Advance action. On commit,
revalidate roster, progress, permissions and destination; move to the correct
map/briefing or fixed next Cup leg. Obtain/retain only the mode consent that
still actually matches, avoiding a duplicate identical mode confirmation.
Event Ready for the final course/rules/build is still required before Start.
No automatic race because six people chose 'Next'.

Any ServiceBreak immediately selects a shared service intermission. The
requesting player can open Garage; others choose their own Garage, meet,
record review, toy or quiet menu. Do not drag everybody through a forced shop
animation, and do not label the requesting player as a troublemaker.

Undecided is never Continue. After approximately 30 seconds, reduce the poll to
a calm persistent status/intermission strip rather than flashing a punitive
timer. No auto-ready, auto-kick, spending requirement or delay penalty. A player
can change their choice before the leader commits; repeated changes are bounded
against message spam but ordinary reconsideration is allowed.

7.2 Returning from service

Retain the proposed next stage/event and its readable preparation summary.
People finish tuning, resolve or safely save their local drafts, and explicitly
choose 'Ready to Continue'. Once all current members agree, the leader advances
to that shared briefing. A ready player can keep playing an interruptible toy.
Members who already chose Continue retain that still-valid DESTINATION consent
through the service break; do not make them repeat it merely because somebody
else opened Garage. That does not preserve obsolete Event Ready or approve a
changed course/build/rules revision. The main-event start automatically pauses
those toys under section 1.

Requesting service means 'I need time', not 'everybody must remain in Garage'.
No force-start can override an active member's refusal. A person genuinely
leaving can explicitly leave the convoy; disconnect rules remain server-owned.

Preserve useful consent when the proposal is unchanged. Do not reset mode
agreement because someone opens Cap Clash, and do not count cosmetic or audio
changes as altered race rules. Real roster/mode/eligible-frontier changes are
new proposals; revalidate affected consents and explain the change once.

7.3 Important cases

- Mixed campaign outcomes: settle individual clears first. The next eligible
  shared frontier may be the stage just attempted. Show who still needs it
  neutrally; never skip them ahead or award a nonexistent completion.
- Pending rewards/access: show settlement pending. Keep safe browsing/toys
  available, but do not spend unsettled currency or assume a future unlock.
- Normal finale: show campaign completion and Return/Hard availability. Do not
  silently begin Hard; its explicit intent and all-member eligibility apply.
- Hard finale: no nonexistent S31. Offer return, replay/selection and Freeplay.
- Challenges: Continue returns to a real eligible selection or known authored
  sequence; it does not invent a random challenge target.
- Freeplay: existing direct/vote selection applies. A service vote is not a
  substitute for the existing timed course ballot or finalized Event Ready.
- Cup: preserve the schedule and Cup state across intermission. Enforce the
  preannounced between-leg build restrictions. Testing a candidate is allowed
  privately, but an illegal change cannot enter a build-locked Cup leg.
- Leader disconnect/transfer: retain results, toys and proposed destination;
  apply the existing leadership epoch and revalidate consent. No forced lobby
  reset, replayed rewards or resurrection of a DQ entrant.
- Solo: the same choices work immediately without a poll timer or fictitious
  votes; direct confirmation still clearly identifies the next action.

8. MEANINGFUL UPGRADES WHILE KEEPING A FAVORITE CAR

8.1 Campaign balance contract

Every one of the 18 authored models must have a legal viable build path for
Normal and Hard; any starting car can remain the player's long-term project.
Do not force buying a different make/model or losing a favorite livery. Preserve
handling identity, drive layout and meaningful tradeoffs; upgrades should not
turn every chassis into the same high-power all-purpose vehicle.

At the same time, unchanged starter mechanical performance is not the intended
whole-campaign solution. The curve must make real component improvements useful
and normally necessary for representative players, not merely suggest buying
cosmetics or turning an unused 'power' bar green.

Use the existing four Normal acts as preparation bands. Target at least three
substantial, affordable performance-build revisions across a starter's Normal
journey, plus repeated route-appropriate tuning/loadout decisions. A meaningful
workshop decision roughly every three or four regular stages is a pacing target,
not an enforced stop, purchase quota or demand to buy a full new setup each time.
Hard requires a well-developed car and further appropriate tuning/parts where
needed; not an automatic reset or a second mandatory garage purchase.

Initial direction: fundamentals such as tyres/braking/usable gearing first;
stronger balanced T2/T3 combinations through later Normal acts; selective higher-
tier parts for late/Hard goals where the tested economy supports them. Do not
require top-tier parts in every slot. Use measured performance and the authored
course/conditions, not part-price totals, to set reference builds and targets.

Changing a part must affect actual acceleration, braking, corner response,
traction, gearing, stability or another implemented parameter. Verify the
tradeoff against the same input/controller conditions. Cosmetics and utility
score bonuses cannot satisfy the mechanical development requirement.

No hidden 'has spent X', installed-upgrade-count gate, compulsory replacement
car, time lock, wear/damage bill, randomized part drop, or artificial downgrade
of a skilled player's valid performance. An exceptional legal stock clear is
not a bug to punish. The intended curve is established by representative
calibration, not by rejecting an honest finish because it was too inexpensive.

8.2 Budget and accessibility guardrails

Preserve the existing Credits and parts ceilings, losing-finisher income,
no-quit payout, server-owned transactions and the 15,000-RP budget. Do not fund
required upgrades through toys, Test Yard farming, or free-preview loopholes.

Model progression with ordinary race income, first clears, a realistic loss
rate and optional cosmetics/course purchases. Recommended affordable builds
must be purchasable before their targeted campaign demand. Show at least a
low-cost incremental solution and a longer-term alternative where possible.

Retain class-legal campaign loaners as a fallback against poverty/legality
softlocks, not the default build used to claim 'your favorite car is viable'.
The owned favorite-car route must work on its own. The loaner may have upgraded
hardware that the player has not purchased; label it clearly and do not grant
ownership. Do not remove this safety feature without a later user decision.

8.3 Useful preparation feedback

Results/next-stage briefing should give an interpretable driving problem:
short straights and slow exits; wet braking; sustained cornering; repeated
uphill acceleration; stability over crests; or top-speed gearing.

Where evidence exists, use measured telemetry: repeated redline before braking,
long braking distance on the test protocol, loss of traction under throttle,
or inconsistent entry speeds. Separate driver technique from part capability.
Do not claim a specific suspension flaw without measuring/modeling it. Use
'possible cause / try comparing' where the diagnosis is uncertain.

Suggested builds are explained options, never auto-purchases or an optimizer
that replaces a player's taste. Link to the relevant subsystem, a saved loadout,
a comparison, or the Test Yard. Offer stock/current/loaner/owned-car routes with
honest compatibility and cost, not 'your car number is too low' alone.

9. PER-CAR LOADOUTS, REFERENCES AND SAFE RESTORATION

9.1 Scope and capacity

Provide at least eight named MECHANICAL loadout slots PER OWNED VEHICLE INSTANCE,
not eight globally and not one shared list for every car of the same model.
Two owned instances of the same model have independent applied state, names and
loadouts. Compatible blueprint copying can be explicit, but does not duplicate
parts, ownership or a whole account.

Retain at least five independent visual presets per owned car. Mechanical
loadouts do not unexpectedly overwrite paint, decals, body appearance or a
friend's suggested style. A race-preset shortcut may deliberately reference
both a mechanical and a visual preset, but its composition is visible.

Each mechanical loadout records stable car instance/model IDs, selected owned
part IDs by slot, tuning values and version, mechanical utility choice,
performance-relevant appearance components where applicable, derived PI/class,
validated content/handling version, a note and last updated time. Store IDs,
not array positions. Derived stats must be recomputed/validated after changes.

Do not hide performance-relevant selections in the visual-only system. Purely
visual changes leave mechanical setup intact. If a body/wheel/aero variant has
an actual physics effect, its restore consequences are explicit and validated.

Driving aids, input bindings, audio and accessibility settings are NOT silently
changed by a loadout. Record them as test context where they affect performance;
changing them requires their own normal settings action and record tags.

9.2 Operations and state labels

Support Save As, Rename, Overwrite with confirmation, Duplicate, Delete with
confirmation, Compare, Load into Draft, and Apply. Show the current applied
loadout, a dirty unsaved candidate, and last-raced reference distinctly.
Examples of user labels: 'Wet Grip', 'Short Gears', 'Drift Setup', 'Boss Try'.
Do not auto-populate empty slots with invented owned builds or confusing names.

Allow up to three pinned mechanical presets per car in a quick selector on
Garage and the event briefing when build changes are permitted. Show class/cap
compatibility before selection. An owned legal preset can be applied as one
whole-build action without reopening every parts category. Warn before replacing
a dirty draft, never auto-buy missing parts, and invalidate only the relevant
readiness. The selector cannot bypass a frozen event or build-locked Cup rule.

Loading into Draft previews the full setup at once without spending or changing
the active race build. Applying replaces the entire legal owned component/tune
set atomically, not one frame or HTTP call per part. Show a compact delta list
and resulting PI/class. Reusing already owned parts is free and does not consume
them; maintain existing chassis compatibility and ownership rules.

If an old/shared draft references a missing, unowned, locked, removed or
incompatible part, show exactly which. Do not silently substitute stock, buy
the missing items, mark ready with a partial build, or erase the saved preset.
A legal stock fallback is a separate explicit choice. A blueprint may be saved
as a planning draft with 'needs parts' status without granting ownership.

9.3 Automatic recovery snapshots

In addition to the eight user slots, maintain bounded protected references:
- 'Before Workshop': the entire applied mechanical state on entry into this
  editing session, until the player explicitly ends that workshop session or
  accepts a new baseline. Testing, toys, temporary menu changes and a reconnect
  do not silently replace this reference with the current experiment.
- 'Before Last Apply': the prior applied state before the most recent successful
  change. Updated only after a successful atomic apply, not on every preview.
- 'Last Race Build': the exact frozen mechanical build actually used in the last
  full-size event. Record it only when that player actually begins authorized
  driving, not when a proposal is opened or a load fails. Testing, a slot-car lap,
  or opening a menu never replaces it.

Restore any reference through preview/validation and one deliberate Apply.
A restore does not refund a legitimate purchase, unequip another car's items,
remove ownership, rewind race results, change earned currency or overwrite
livery. If a version change makes a reference invalid, preserve it with a clear
repair explanation rather than deleting it or lying that restoration worked.

Loadouts and protected references persist across normal restarts within their
respective Local/Online profiles. Use atomic versioned writes and conflict
checks for two clients editing the same car. A stale editor must not overwrite
another device's accepted setup or spend a wallet twice.

10. GARAGE TEST YARD — DRIVE IT BEFORE COMMITTING

10.1 Required facility, not just a dyno animation

Add 'Test Current' and 'Test Candidate' directly to Garage. The primary purpose
is to FEEL the same car controller that will enter a race, not to watch animated
stat bars or trust an automated lap. The player steers, accelerates, brakes,
changes gear and drifts with their real control bindings and driving aids.

Build one small, deliberately authored service facility. Reuse suitable tutorial
assets/geometry if that creates a clear, independently accessible area; do not
require replaying T00 or another lesson. It does not count as a 30th race course.

Required elements:
- A safe launch/braking straight, roughly 250–300 metres where practical.
- A skid pad supporting left and right sustained turns and drift recovery.
- A short slalom and a tightening/decreasing-radius corner.
- A connected small loop with at least one elevation/camber change and a clear
  return/reset point. Around 700–1,000 metres of connected driving is an initial
  authoring target, not a mandatory race length or promise of top-speed testing.
- Marked optional precision-stop boxes for 'Brake for the Box' style practice.
- Dry and wet presets using the SAME material/grip rules as the corresponding
  full-size conditions, with an explicit reset when the surface changes.

No pedestrians, public-meet cars, traffic or friendly AI obstruct the test.
No visible/mechanical damage, repair bill, entry fee, reward, official finish,
championship progress or challenge completion. Local/offline entry works without
an external server. Online testing remains private; it uses trusted configuration
inputs but does not pretend local practice is a verified authoritative result.

The full race course remains the definitive test: this compact yard cannot
prove long high-speed stability, every surface, race traffic, all elevations,
competitive performance or latency-dependent contacts. Label its limitations.
Do not hide the player's option to later test on an already available full route.

10.2 Same simulation and comparable conditions

Use the same vehicle/controller code, tuning interpretation, steering/assist
logic, physics step, collision shape and applicable surface parameters as the
main game. Do not add hidden grip, artificial stabilization, a better input
filter or a different arcade model just to make the showroom demo attractive.
Keep visual toy physics strictly separate from this full-size car test.

Initialize all test variables that affect feel consistently: build hash,
physics/content version, surface/weather preset, start position and direction,
velocity, wheel/contact state, relevant temperatures/loads if modeled, camera,
input device and enabled assists. Report frame/input/network-context differences
instead of claiming a private local run predicts online latency perfectly.

Useful optional overlay: speed/gear, braking/acceleration timing, slip-angle
estimate, steering/throttle/brake traces, start/stop distance, and clean slalom
or corner-exit results when actually measured. Never fabricate top speed from a
straight that is too short; show 'not reached in this test length'.

10.3 A/B comparison without losing the good setup

A = selected baseline, normally Current Applied or Last Race Build.
B = the candidate currently in the Garage draft, including all chosen parts
and tuning. Freeze both references for the comparison so changing a library
preset elsewhere does not silently alter the active test.

Allow quick switching with a clear 'Reset and drive A/B' action. Switch only at
a stopped safe state or by explicitly resetting to the same start; never swap
mass/tyres/gearing at speed midcorner. Clear transient dynamics consistently.

Preserve separately labeled A/B observations and at least the last three valid
runs for each in the current comparison. Different conditions/assists/version
are incomparable by default; do not display a misleading percentage improvement.
Keep a qualitative notes field and 'prefer A / prefer B' marker locally; subjective
feel must not be overruled by one generated composite score.

The user can change a component/tune, return to the yard, and compare again
without navigating a long inventory or losing the baseline. Back returns to
Garage with the candidate intact. Provide 'Apply candidate', 'Keep as draft',
'Restore baseline', and normal loadout actions with clear consequences.

10.4 Test before purchase

Allow candidate previews of compatible, currently shop-unlocked parts the player
does not yet own, including an unaffordable but visible part. Label them
'Preview only — not owned'. No purchase, debit, reward or online entitlement
occurs merely by selecting Test. Hidden/locked story equipment need not be exposed.

Use actual part parameters and appearance where relevant. After testing, present
the exact missing-part list and cost. 'Buy and Apply' requires an explicit quote
and confirmation; recheck wallet, price revision, availability, compatibility
and ownership at the authoritative transaction. Owned parts must not be bought
again. Failure leaves the original applied car intact and the planning draft
available. Repeated requests cannot charge or grant twice.

A preview car cannot enter a public meet as an owned build, join a race, satisfy
a mastery predicate, generate a verified record, or persist as unlocked inventory.
The client cannot remove a 'preview' flag to convert it into a legal car; the
server independently reconstructs/validates accepted race and display builds.

Trying alternatives is free. This is not a rental economy, another currency,
an account credit line, a refunded auto-purchase, or timed paid test access.

10.5 Convoy behavior and safe interruption

Garage editing and candidate testing are preparation, not a hidden race. Show
'Garage — testing candidate' as actual activity while retaining Intent. The
player remains unready for any event whose validated build they are changing.
Others can use the five toys; no one gets pulled into the Test Yard with them.

A separate 'Test ready build' path is read-only: use EXACTLY the currently
accepted applied build, no component swap, no candidate/performance edits. The
player can remain Event Ready, with clear warning that the main launch interrupts
practice. Opening a different candidate prompts the deliberate Unready action
before doing so. Never invalidate the whole convoy solely for a local test.

On valid event allocation, return that read-only tester automatically to the
main loading flow. Preserve useful local test notes, clear input, release the
private test vehicle/scene, and enter the frozen accepted race build. No mandatory
lap completion, park-back animation, exit dialogue or test results screen.
Do not mark Main Load Ready until the real track/player state is actually ready.

Candidate drafts do not have to be discarded to enter a mode selection screen:
store them without applying and obtain explicit appropriate consent. They cannot
be silently applied on selection, queue resolution or start. All real purchases
must already have reached an authoritative outcome before claiming race legality.

10.6 Loading and resource budgets

The normal Garage shell and control/presence connection stay alive. Use compact
assets, caching and a private/additive test scene or another appropriate existing
Unity technique. Verify the selected method against the installed engine and
actual project; no assumption that scene-loading calls alone isolate physics.

A warm open/close target of roughly two seconds and a small bounded cold load
are goals to measure, not universal performance claims. Show real progress when
needed; allow cancel safely. Test from the standalone player, not only a warmed
Editor. Avoid loading a whole full-size region for a slalom or keeping all five
toy renderers alive beneath the Test Yard.

After repeated A/B resets and Garage returns there must be no accumulating cars,
colliders, AudioSources, render textures, event subscriptions or input handlers.
Incoming readiness/convoy messages remain responsive throughout the load. No
second player-controlled full-size car may leak into the networked world.

Persist compact recoverable snapshots across restarts/dormancy. Input/transform

FINAL ACCEPTANCE PRINCIPLE
A waiting player can choose something genuinely fun, stay ready, and leave it
instantly without losing the evening's progress. A tuning player can save what
works, feel what might work, restore what did work, and enter the next race
with an informed choice. Neither person becomes a hostage to the other's UI.

through the complete revised Night Signal assignment.
